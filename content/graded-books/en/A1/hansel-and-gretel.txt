Chapter 1: No More Bread

At the edge of a big forest, there is a small house. A poor woodcutter lives in this house. He lives with his wife and his two children. The boy is Hansel. The girl is Gretel. Hansel is ten years old. Gretel is nine. They are good children, and they love their father very much.

The woman is not their real mother. Their mother is dead. This woman is their stepmother. She does not love the children. She thinks only about herself.

Every day, the father goes into the forest. He cuts trees with his big axe. Then he sells the wood in the town. But this year is very bad. There is no rain. The fields are dry and brown. Nobody has money for wood. And nobody has food.

In the little house, there is almost nothing to eat. One evening, the family eats a little soup. It is only water and two old potatoes. After dinner, the children go to bed. But they cannot sleep. Their stomachs are empty.

In the kitchen, the father sits by the fire. He looks very sad.

"What can we do?" he says to his wife. "We have no food for our children. We have no food for us."

The stepmother looks at him with cold eyes. "I have a plan," she says. "Tomorrow morning, we take the children into the forest. We go very deep into the trees. We make a fire for them. We give them one piece of bread. Then we go to work and leave them there. They cannot find the way home. Then we have two mouths less to feed."

"No!" says the father. "I cannot do that! They are my children. The wild animals in the forest can eat them!"

"Then all four of us die of hunger," says the woman. "Is that better? You are a fool."

She talks and talks. She does not stop. At last, the poor father says yes. He is very unhappy. He does not sleep all night.

Chapter 2: The White Stones

But the children are not asleep. They hear everything. Gretel starts to cry.

"Oh, Hansel," she says quietly. "What can we do? We are lost!"

"Shh, Gretel," says Hansel. "Do not cry. Do not be afraid. I have an idea."

Hansel waits. Soon the father and the stepmother are asleep. Then he gets out of bed. He puts on his old jacket. He opens the door very quietly. He goes out into the garden.

The moon is big and bright tonight. In front of the house, there are many little white stones. They shine in the moonlight like silver coins. Hansel picks up the stones. He fills all his pockets with them. Then he goes back to bed.

"Sleep now, little sister," he says. "Everything is all right."

Early in the morning, the stepmother wakes them up. The sun is not in the sky yet.

"Get up, lazy children!" she says. "We go into the forest today. We must get wood."

She gives each child a small piece of bread. "This is your lunch," she says. "Do not eat it now. There is nothing more."

Gretel puts the bread in her apron. Hansel cannot put it in his pockets. His pockets are full of stones.

The family walks into the forest. Hansel walks at the back. Every few steps, he stops. He turns around and looks at the house.

"Hansel, what are you doing?" says the father. "Why do you stop all the time? Walk with us."

"I look at my white cat," says Hansel. "She sits on the roof. She wants to say goodbye to me."

"Silly boy," says the stepmother. "That is not your cat. That is the morning sun on the chimney."

But Hansel does not look at the house. Each time, he takes a white stone from his pocket. He drops it on the path. One stone, then another stone, then another. He makes a long line of stones.

Deep in the forest, they stop. The father makes a big fire.

"Sit by the fire, children," says the stepmother. "Rest here. We go to cut wood. Later we come back for you."

The children sit by the fire. At midday, they eat their bread. They can hear an axe. "Our father is near," they think. But it is not an axe. It is only a branch. The wind moves it against a tree. Bang, bang, bang.

The children are very tired. They close their eyes and fall asleep.

Chapter 3: The Way Home

When they open their eyes, it is night. The fire is dead. It is cold and very dark. Gretel starts to cry again.

"How can we get out of the forest?" she says.

"Wait a little," says Hansel. "Wait for the moon. Then we can find the way."

Soon the moon comes up over the trees. Hansel takes his sister's hand. On the path, the white stones shine like new silver coins. They show the way home.

The children walk all night. They follow the stones, one by one. At last, in the early morning, they see their house. They knock on the door.

The stepmother opens it. She is very surprised.

"Bad children!" she says. "Why do you sleep so long in the forest? We think you never want to come back!"

But the father is very happy. He hugs his children. He does not want to lose them again.

For some weeks, there is a little food. Life is a bit better. But then the food is gone again. One night, the children hear the stepmother again.

"There is only half a loaf of bread," she says. "After that, nothing. The children must go. This time, we take them deeper into the forest. They cannot find the way out."

The father is very sad. "It is better to share our last bread with them," he says. But the woman does not listen. And again, he says yes.

When the parents are asleep, Hansel gets up again. He goes to the door. But the door does not open. The stepmother has the key! Hansel cannot get out to the stones.

"Do not cry, Gretel," he says. "We can find a way."

In the morning, the stepmother gives the children their bread. It is smaller than the last time.

They walk into the forest again. Hansel walks at the back. He breaks his bread into very small pieces in his pocket. Every few steps, he stops. He drops a piece of bread on the path.

"Hansel, why do you stop?" says the father.

"I look at my little white bird," says Hansel. "It sits on the roof. It wants to say goodbye."

"Silly boy," says the stepmother. "That is the sun on the chimney again. Walk!"

They walk very far. They go deep into the forest. This part of the forest is new for the children. Again, the father makes a fire.

"Stay here," says the stepmother. "We come back in the evening."

At midday, Gretel shares her bread with Hansel. Then the children sleep. Nobody comes back for them.

Chapter 4: Lost in the Forest

When the children wake up, it is dark. Hansel holds Gretel's hand.

"Wait for the moon," he says. "Then we can see the bread on the path."

The moon comes up. The children look for the bread. But they cannot find it. Not one piece! Where is it? The birds of the forest are hungry too. They see the bread. They eat every piece of it.

"We can find the way without the bread," says Hansel. But he is not sure.

They walk all night. They walk all the next day. But they do not find the way out. The forest is very big. All the trees look the same. They have nothing to eat. They find only a few berries on the ground.

The children are very hungry and very tired. Their legs hurt. At night, they lie down under a tree. They sleep on the cold ground.

But the forest at night is full of noises. An owl calls from a tree. "Hoo, hoo!" Something moves in the bushes. Two yellow eyes look at them from the dark.

"Hansel," says Gretel. "Is that a wolf?"

"No," says Hansel. "It is only a fox. Foxes are afraid of people. Close your eyes, little sister."

He puts his jacket over Gretel. He sings her an old song. It is the song of their real mother. Soon Gretel is asleep. But Hansel stays awake for a long time. He listens to the forest. He thinks about his father.

"Does he miss us?" he thinks. "Does he look for us?"

At last, he sleeps too.

On the third morning, they walk again. But they go deeper and deeper into the forest.

"Hansel," says Gretel. "I cannot walk much more."

At midday, they see a beautiful white bird. It sits on a branch. It sings a lovely song. The children stop and listen.

Then the bird opens its wings. It flies a little way. Then it stops and looks at them. It flies again, and then it stops again.

"It wants us to follow it," says Gretel.

The children follow the white bird through the trees. After a while, they come to a little house. The bird sits on the roof.

The children look at the house. They cannot believe their eyes.

Chapter 5: The House of Sweets

The house is not made of wood. It is not made of stone. The walls are made of gingerbread. The roof is made of cake. The windows are made of clear sugar. The door is made of chocolate. There are sweets everywhere, red and green and yellow.

"Look, Gretel!" says Hansel. "Food! A whole house of food!"

He is very, very hungry. He breaks a piece off the roof. He puts it in his mouth. It is sweet and soft. It is the best cake in the world.

Gretel goes to the window. She breaks off a piece of sugar. It is cold and sweet, like ice.

Then a little voice comes from inside the house:

"Nibble, nibble, like a mouse. Who is nibbling at my house?"

The children are a little afraid. But they are so hungry. Gretel answers:

"It is the wind, the wind so wild. It is the wind, the heaven's child."

They eat more and more. Hansel takes a big piece of the roof. Gretel takes a whole round window.

Then the chocolate door opens. An old woman comes out. She is very, very old. She walks with a stick. Her back is bent. Her eyes are red, and she cannot see well. She has a long nose and only one tooth.

The children drop their food. They want to run away.

"Oh, dear children," says the old woman. Her voice is soft and kind. "Do not be afraid. You are hungry. Come in, come in. Nobody hurts you here."

She takes them by the hand. She takes them into her house. Inside, there is a table full of good food. There is warm milk. There are pancakes with sugar. There are apples and nuts.

The children eat and eat. It is the best dinner of their lives.

After dinner, the old woman shows them two little beds. The beds have clean white sheets. Hansel and Gretel lie down. They feel safe and warm.

"We are in heaven," they think.

But they are not in heaven. The old woman is not a kind woman. She is a witch! She builds the house of sweets for one reason. She wants to catch children. When she has a child, she cooks it. Then she eats it. That is a special day for her.

The witch cannot see well. But she has a very good nose, like an animal. She can smell people from far away.

At the window, she smiles a terrible smile.

"I have two of them," she says quietly. "They cannot run away now."

Chapter 6: The Witch's Plan

Early in the morning, the witch gets up. The children are still asleep. She looks at their round, red faces.

"What a good dinner they can be!" she says.

She takes Hansel with her bony hands. He wakes up and shouts. But she is very strong. She carries him outside to a small cage. She puts him in the cage. She closes the door with a big lock. Hansel shouts and shouts, but nobody can help him.

Then the witch goes to Gretel. She shakes her.

"Get up, lazy girl!" she says. Now her voice is not kind. "Get water and cook something good for your brother. He is in the cage outside. He must eat a lot. He must get fat. When he is fat, I eat him!"

Gretel cries and cries. But she cannot do anything. She must do what the witch says.

Every day, Gretel cooks the best food for Hansel. He eats meat, eggs and cakes. But Gretel gets nothing. She eats only dry bread and a little water.

Every morning, the witch goes to the cage.

"Hansel," she says, "put out your finger. I want to feel it. Is it fat yet?"

But Hansel is clever. He has a little old bone from his dinner. He puts the bone out of the cage. The witch cannot see well. She feels the bone. It is thin and hard.

"Still so thin!" she says. "Why does he not get fat?"

Four weeks go by. Every day, Hansel shows the witch the bone. Every day, the bone is still thin. At last, the witch gets angry.

"I do not want to wait anymore!" she says. "Fat or thin, tomorrow I cook him!"

Poor Gretel cries all night. "Please, God, help us," she says. "In the forest, the animals can eat us. But at least then we die together."

"Stop that noise," says the witch. "It does not help you."

Chapter 7: The Oven

Early the next morning, Gretel must fill the big pot with water. She must light the fire.

"First we bake bread," says the witch. "The oven is hot now."

She pushes Gretel to the big oven. The fire inside is red and yellow. It is very hot.

"Climb in," says the witch. "Look inside. Is it hot enough for the bread?"

Gretel understands the plan. The witch wants to close the door. She wants to cook Gretel in the oven! Gretel thinks very fast.

"I do not know how to do it," she says. "How do I get in there? Can you show me?"

"Stupid girl!" says the witch. "The door is big enough. Look, I can get in myself!"

She walks to the oven. She bends down and puts her head inside.

Gretel runs at her. She pushes the witch with all her power. The witch falls into the oven! Gretel closes the iron door. She closes it with the lock.

The witch screams and screams. Then everything is quiet.

Gretel runs out of the house. She runs to the cage. She finds the key and opens the door.

"Hansel!" she shouts. "We are free! The old witch is dead!"

Hansel jumps out of the cage like a little bird. The children hug each other. They dance and jump. They laugh and cry at the same time.

Now they are not afraid. They go into the witch's house. In every corner, there are big boxes. The children open the boxes. They are full of pearls and gold and beautiful stones.

"These are better than my white stones," says Hansel. He fills his pockets.

"I want some too," says Gretel. She fills her apron.

"Now let us go," says Hansel. "Let us go out of this forest."

Chapter 8: The White Duck

The children walk for many hours. They come to a big lake. The water is wide and deep.

"We cannot get over this water," says Hansel. "There is no bridge."

"There is no boat either," says Gretel. Then she sees something. "Look! A white duck. Maybe it can help us."

She calls to the duck:

"Little duck, little duck, please help us today. We have no bridge, and we cannot find the way!"

The duck swims to them. It is a big white duck. Hansel sits on its back.

"Come, Gretel," he says. "Sit with me."

"No," says Gretel. "We are too heavy for the duck. It can take us one by one."

The good duck carries Hansel over the lake. Then it comes back for Gretel. The children thank the duck and walk on.

The sun is warm now. Birds sing in the trees. The children are not tired anymore. They are happy, and they walk fast.

"Hansel," says Gretel, "what about our stepmother? She does not want us. Maybe she closes the door. Maybe she sends us back to the forest."

Hansel thinks about it. "Now we have gold," he says. "She likes gold very much. Maybe she lets us stay."

"I do not want to live with her," says Gretel. "But I want to see Father."

"Me too," says Hansel. He holds her hand. "Come on. We are almost there."

Now the forest looks more and more familiar. They know these trees. They know this path. And then, at last, they see it. Their father's little house!

The children run. They open the door and run inside.

Their father sits at the table. He looks old and sad. Every day without his children is a sad day for him. When he sees his children, he jumps up. He holds them in his arms. He cries with joy.

"My children!" he says. "My dear children! Can you forgive me?"

"Yes, Father," says Gretel. "We love you."

"Where is our stepmother?" asks Hansel.

"She is dead," says the father. "There is nobody here now. Only me. And now you."

Gretel opens her apron. Pearls and gold fall onto the table. Hansel empties his pockets. More gold falls on the table.

The father cannot believe it. "Is this real?" he says.

"It is real," says Hansel. "Now we are not poor anymore."

From that day, the family has enough food. They buy bread, meat and fruit. They never go to bed hungry again. The father does not need to cut wood every day. He sits with his children by the fire. They tell him about the white stones, the house of sweets and the witch.

And sometimes, a white bird sits on their roof. It sings a lovely song.

The End
