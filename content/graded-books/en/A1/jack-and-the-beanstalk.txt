Chapter 1: Milky-White

In a small village, there is a little old house. A poor woman lives in this house with her son. The boy's name is Jack. Jack is a good boy, but he is lazy. He does not like to work. He likes to sit in the sun, look at the clouds and sing songs.

Jack and his mother have no money. They have a very small garden, one old table, one old bed and one cow. Her name is Milky-White.

Milky-White is a good cow. Every morning, she gives them milk. Jack's mother takes the milk to the market and sells it there. With the money, she buys bread and cheese. That is all they have.

But one morning, there is a big problem. Jack's mother goes to the cow with her bucket. She sits down and tries and tries. But there is no milk. Not one drop.

"Oh no," she says. "Milky-White is too old. She has no more milk for us."

She goes back into the house and sits at the old table. She puts her head in her hands.

"Jack," she says, "what can we do? We have no milk, no money and no bread. Tomorrow we have nothing to eat."

"I can find work, Mother," says Jack. "I can work on a farm."

His mother laughs a sad little laugh. "You, Jack? You do not like work, and work does not like you."

She looks out of the window at the cow for a long time.

"We must sell Milky-White," she says. "There is no other way. With the money, we can open a little shop."

Jack is sad, because he loves Milky-White. He talks to her every day. But his mother is right.

"All right, Mother," he says. "I can take her to the market today."

"Good boy," says his mother. "But be clever, Jack. Milky-White is a good cow. Get a good price for her. Do you understand?"

"Yes, Mother. Do not worry."

Jack puts a rope around the cow's neck. He gives his mother a kiss, and he walks out of the gate with Milky-White.

"Come on, old girl," he says. "We go to the market today."

"Moo," says the cow. She does not want to go, but she walks with Jack down the road.

Chapter 2: The Funny Little Man

Jack and Milky-White walk down the long road. The sun is warm, and the birds sing in the trees. Jack sings too. He thinks about the market and the money.

After an hour, Jack sees a strange little man at the side of the road. He is old, with a long white beard and a funny green hat. His eyes are small and bright, and they smile.

"Good morning, Jack," says the little man.

Jack stops. "Good morning. How do you know my name?"

The little man only smiles. "Where do you go with that cow, Jack?"

"I go to the market," says Jack. "I want to sell her."

"Ah," says the man. "You look like a clever boy. Tell me, Jack. How many beans make five?"

Jack thinks. This is easy. "Two beans in each hand and one in your mouth," he says. "That makes five."

The little man laughs. "Very good! You are a very clever boy. And here they are."

He opens his hand. In his hand, there are five beans. They are not brown or green. They are red, blue, purple and gold, and they shine in the sun.

"Oh!" says Jack. "They are beautiful!"

"They are not just beautiful," says the little man. "They are magic beans. Put them in the ground tonight, and tomorrow morning they grow up to the sky."

"Up to the sky?" says Jack. "Really?"

"Really," says the man. "I want your cow, and you want my beans. So let us change. My beans for your cow. And if they do not grow, you can have your cow back."

Jack looks at the beans, and he looks at Milky-White. He thinks about his mother and the gold coins. But the beans are so beautiful. And they are magic!

"Yes," says Jack with a big smile. "That is a good deal."

He gives the rope to the little man, and the little man gives him the five beans. Jack puts them carefully in his pocket.

"Goodbye, Milky-White," says Jack. He puts his face on her neck. "Be a good girl."

"Moo," says the cow.

The little man takes the cow and walks away. After a few steps, Jack turns around. But the road is empty. There is no little man and no cow. There is only the wind in the trees.

"That is strange," says Jack. Then he puts his hand in his pocket. The beans are still there. He smiles and runs home.

Chapter 3: The Magic Beans

Jack's mother stands at the door. She sees him from far away, and she sees that he has no cow with him.

"Jack! You are back so soon!" she says. "How much money do you have? Five gold coins? Ten?"

"Better than money, Mother," says Jack. "Much better!"

"Better than money? What is better than money?"

Jack takes the beans out of his pocket and opens his hand. "Look! Five beans!"

His mother looks at the beans, and she looks at Jack. She does not speak. Her face goes white. Then it goes red. Then it goes very, very red.

"Beans?" she says. "You give our cow for five beans?"

"They are magic beans, Mother!" says Jack. "Plant them tonight, and tomorrow they grow up to the sky!"

"Magic beans!" cries his mother. "Oh, you silly, silly boy! Our only cow, for a handful of beans! What can we eat now? Five beans for two people?"

She takes the beans from his hand and opens the window. And she throws the beans out into the garden, one, two, three, four, five.

"There!" she says. "That is the place for your magic beans! And no dinner for you tonight. Not one bite. Go to bed. Now!"

Jack goes up the stairs to his little room under the roof. He is very hungry and very sad.

"My mother is right," he says. "Now we have no cow and no food. And it is all my fault."

He lies on his bed for a long time. But at last, he closes his eyes and sleeps.

That night, something strange happens in the garden. Under the moon, five little green shoots come up out of the earth. They grow and grow. They turn around each other like a rope. They grow past the window and past the roof, up and up and up into the dark night sky.

In the morning, Jack opens his eyes. His room is very dark. There is something big and green in front of his window.

"What is that?" he says.

He jumps out of bed and runs to the window. And he cannot believe his eyes.

In the garden, there is a beanstalk. It is as thick as a tree, and its leaves are as big as umbrellas. Jack looks up. The beanstalk goes up and up and up into the clouds. He cannot see the top.

"The beans!" says Jack. "They really are magic!"

Chapter 4: Up, Up, Up

The beanstalk is right next to Jack's window. It is like a big green ladder.

"I want to know what is at the top," says Jack. "And I can climb it. It is easy."

He climbs out of the window, puts his feet on the big leaves, and starts to climb. He climbs and climbs, past the roof of his house and past the tops of the trees. He looks down. The people in the street are as small as ants.

His arms hurt, his legs hurt, and the wind is cold. But he does not stop. At last, he climbs into the clouds. Everything is white and wet around him. Then his head comes out of the clouds, and he sees the bright blue sky.

Jack climbs off the beanstalk. He is in a strange country. The ground is soft and white, like snow, but it is not cold. There are no trees and no flowers. There is only a long, straight road.

At last, he sees a house at the end of the road. It is a great big tall house. The door is as high as a church, and the windows are as big as fields.

On the doorstep, there is a great big tall woman. Her shoes are as big as boats, and her hands are as big as tables.

Jack is afraid, but he is very hungry too. He walks up to her.

"Good morning, madam," he says politely. "Please, can I have some breakfast?"

The big woman looks down at him. "Breakfast? You must go away, little boy! My husband is a giant, and he eats boys. He likes boys on toast for his breakfast. You must not be here when he comes home."

"Oh, please, madam," says Jack. "I have nothing in my stomach since yesterday. Maybe I die of hunger, and then I am dead in any case."

The giant's wife is not a bad woman. She feels sorry for the little boy.

"Oh, all right," she says. "Come into the kitchen. Quick!"

In the enormous kitchen, she gives him a big piece of bread and cheese and a cup of milk. The cup is as big as a bucket. Jack eats and eats. It is the best breakfast of his life.

But then there is a noise. Thump! Thump! Thump! The floor moves, and the plates on the shelf jump.

"Oh no!" says the giant's wife. "That is my husband! He comes home!"

Chapter 5: Fee-Fi-Fo-Fum

Thump! Thump! Thump! The whole house shakes.

"Quick, little boy!" says the giant's wife. "Hide in here!"

She opens the big oven. It is cold inside. Jack jumps in, and she closes the door, but not completely. Jack can see out through a little space.

The kitchen door opens, and the giant comes in. He is enormous. He has a big red face, a big black beard and big yellow teeth. In his hand, he has three dead cows on a rope.

"Wife!" he shouts. "Cook these cows for my breakfast!" Then he stops. He lifts his big nose into the air. Sniff, sniff, sniff.

"Fee-fi-fo-fum!
I smell the blood of an Englishman.
Is he alive, or is he dead?
I want his bones to make my bread!"

In the oven, Jack holds his breath. His heart goes boom, boom, boom.

"Oh, husband, you are silly," says the wife. "You smell the little boy from yesterday. Remember? The boy for your dinner. Now go and wash your hands. Your breakfast is almost ready."

The giant sniffs one more time. Then he goes out to wash.

The giant comes back and sits at the table. He eats the three cows and ten loaves of bread. He drinks a barrel of milk. Jack watches through the little space with big eyes.

After breakfast, the giant takes two big bags out of the cupboard and opens them on the table. They are full of gold coins, and the coins shine in the light.

The giant counts his money. "One, two, three, four, five..." He counts very slowly. His eyes start to close. "...ninety-eight, ninety-nine..."

His head goes down on the table, and the giant sleeps. He snores, and the snores are like thunder. The windows shake.

Jack opens the oven door very quietly and climbs out. He walks on his toes across the kitchen, past the giant's big feet. He climbs onto a chair and onto the table. He takes one bag of gold. It is very heavy.

He puts the bag on his shoulder and runs out of the house, down the long road to the beanstalk. The bag is too heavy for the climb. So Jack throws it down, and it falls through the clouds into his mother's garden. Then Jack climbs down after it, down and down and down.

His mother is in the garden. She looks at the bag, at the beanstalk and at Jack.

"Jack!" she says. "Where are you all morning? And what is this?"

"Look inside, Mother," says Jack.

His mother opens the bag and sees the gold. She sits down on the grass. "Oh, Jack," she says. "Oh, my clever boy!"

Chapter 6: The Hen with the Golden Eggs

Now Jack and his mother are rich. They buy bread, meat and cheese. They buy new clothes, new shoes and new chairs. Jack's mother smiles every day.

But a bag of gold does not last for ever. Some months go by, and the coins get fewer and fewer. One morning, there are only three coins in the bag.

Jack looks out of the window at the beanstalk. "I want to go up again," he says.

"No, Jack!" says his mother. "The giant can eat you!"

"Do not worry, Mother," says Jack. "I am clever now. And I am fast."

Early the next morning, Jack climbs the beanstalk again. He climbs into the clouds and walks along the long road. And there is the great big tall house, and the great big tall woman on the doorstep.

"Good morning, madam," he says. "Please, can I have some breakfast?"

"Go away, boy," says the giant's wife. "Some months ago, a little boy comes here. I give him breakfast, and then my husband's bag of gold is gone. My husband is very angry with me!"

"How strange," says Jack. "Maybe I know something about it. But I am too hungry to talk. Please, just a little bread?"

The wife wants to know more. "All right," she says. "Come in." But before Jack can say anything, there is a noise. Thump! Thump! Thump!

"Into the oven!" says the wife, and Jack jumps in.

The giant comes in and sniffs the air.

"Fee-fi-fo-fum!
I smell the blood of an Englishman.
Is he alive, or is he dead?
I want his bones to make my bread!"

"Oh, husband," says his wife. "It is the bones of your dinner. Sit down and eat."

The giant eats four sheep and twelve cakes. Then he says, "Wife, bring me my hen!"

The wife brings a little brown hen and puts it on the table. It is not big or special. It looks like a normal hen.

"Lay!" says the giant to the hen.

And the hen lays an egg. But it is not a normal egg. It is an egg of pure gold!

"Lay!" says the giant again. And the hen lays another golden egg. Then another, and another.

In the oven, Jack's eyes are as big as plates. "A hen with golden eggs!" he thinks. "With that hen, my mother is never poor again!"

Soon the giant's head goes down on the table, and he snores like thunder.

Jack gets out of the oven and climbs onto the table. He takes the hen under his arm, and he runs!

But the hen does not like this. "Cluck! Cluck! Cluck!" she cries.

The giant opens one eye. "Wife!" he shouts. "Where is my hen?"

But Jack is out of the door already. He runs down the road and climbs down the beanstalk as fast as he can. In the garden, he puts the hen on the grass.

"Lay," he says.

And the little hen lays a golden egg. Jack's mother laughs and laughs.

Chapter 7: The Golden Harp

Now Jack and his mother are very rich. Every day, the hen lays one golden egg, and they sell the eggs in the town. They have a nice new house with a garden full of flowers.

But Jack is not lazy any more. He wants adventures. Every evening, he looks up at the beanstalk. "What other treasures does the giant have?" he asks himself.

"Jack," says his mother, "please do not go up there again. We have everything now."

"Just one more time, Mother," says Jack.

Early one morning, before his mother wakes up, Jack climbs the beanstalk again. He walks along the road to the great big tall house.

This time, he does not go to the door, because the wife is angry about the hen. He waits behind a bush. When the wife goes out for water, Jack runs into the kitchen. But where can he hide?

The giant knows about the oven now. In the corner, Jack sees a big copper pot. He climbs in and puts the lid on top.

Thump! Thump! Thump! The giant and his wife come in.

"Fee-fi-fo-fum!
I smell the blood of an Englishman!" shouts the giant. "Wife, I smell him! I really do!"

"Then look in the oven," says his wife. "Little boys like the oven."

The giant runs to the oven and opens the door. Nothing. He looks under the table. Nothing. He does not look in the copper pot.

"Strange," says the giant. He sits down and eats five pigs and twenty pies. Then he says, "Wife, bring me my golden harp!"

His wife brings a little harp of gold. It is very beautiful. She puts it on the table.

"Sing!" says the giant.

And the harp sings all by itself. Nobody touches it. It is the most beautiful song in the world. The giant listens, and his big eyes close. Soon he snores like thunder. His wife sleeps in her chair too.

Jack climbs out of the copper pot very quietly. He walks across the kitchen on his toes, climbs onto the table and takes the golden harp in his arms.

But the harp can talk! And it calls out in a loud voice:

"Master! Master! Help! A boy has me!"

The giant opens his eyes and sees Jack on the table with the harp.

"YOU!" he shouts. "You are the boy with my gold and my hen! Now I can catch you! Now I can eat you!"

Chapter 8: The Axe

Jack jumps down from the table with the harp in his arms. He runs out of the house and down the long road as fast as he can.

Behind him, the giant runs too. Thump! Thump! Thump! The ground shakes. The giant is slow, but each of his steps is as long as a street.

"Stop, boy!" shouts the giant. "I want your bones for my bread!"

"Master! Master!" cries the harp.

Jack runs and runs. The giant is very near now, and Jack feels his hot breath on his neck. The giant puts out his big hand. But at that moment, Jack comes to the beanstalk. He jumps onto it and starts to climb down.

The giant stops at the top and looks down. The beanstalk moves in the wind, and he does not like it. But he wants his harp. So he puts one big foot on the beanstalk, then the other, and he climbs down after Jack.

The beanstalk shakes and bends. Down and down they go, through the clouds. Now Jack can see his house and his mother in the garden.

"Mother!" he shouts. "The axe! Bring me the axe! Quick!"

His mother looks up. She sees Jack, and then she sees the giant behind him. She runs to the woodshed and comes back with the axe. Jack jumps down onto the grass and takes the axe from her hands.

Above them, the giant comes out of the clouds. First his big legs, then his big body, then his big red face. "I can see you, boy!" he shouts.

Jack lifts the axe and hits the beanstalk. Chop! Chop! Chop! Chop!

The beanstalk shakes and bends. Then, with a great crack, it falls across the garden, across the road and across the field. And the giant falls with it. Down, down, down he comes. CRASH!

The whole village shakes. And the giant does not move again. That is the end of the giant.

Jack and his mother look at each other. Then his mother puts her arms around him. "Oh, Jack," she says. "My brave boy. My clever boy."

Now there is no beanstalk to the sky. But Jack and his mother have everything they need. The hen lays golden eggs every day, and the golden harp sings for them every evening. It likes its new home, because Jack is kind.

Some years later, Jack meets a beautiful princess. She likes the stories about his adventures in the sky. Jack marries her, and they live happily ever after.

Sometimes, on a summer evening, Jack sits in the garden and looks up at the clouds. And he thinks about a funny little man with a green hat and five magic beans.

The End
