Chapter 1: The Miller and His Big Mouth

There is a small old mill near a fast river. The water turns the big wheel, and the wheel turns the stones. The stones make flour.

A poor miller lives in the mill. He has a thin cat, an old horse and one daughter.

The daughter is young and very pretty, with long brown hair and kind eyes. She works all day. She cleans the house, she cooks the soup and she helps her father with the flour.

"Father," she says, "we have no bread today."

"I know," says the miller. "We have no bread and no money. But I can go to town and sell some flour there."

The miller puts two bags of flour on the old horse. He walks and walks. The road is long and the sun is hot. At last, he comes to the town.

The town is big and busy, with shops and houses and many people. In the middle of the town, there is a castle. The king lives there.

Today the king is in the street. He has a gold crown and a red coat, and many people stand and look at him.

The miller wants to be important. He wants the king to see him, so he goes near the king.

"Good morning, Your Majesty!" he says.

The king looks at him. "Who are you? And what do you want?"

"I am the miller from the river," says the miller. His face is red. He does not know what to say. Then he says a very silly thing.

"Your Majesty, I have a very clever daughter. She can spin straw into gold!"

The people in the street stop and look at the miller. Then they look at the king.

Straw is dry and yellow. Farmers put it on the floor for their animals, and it is very cheap. Gold is not cheap.

"Straw into gold?" says the king. His eyes are big now. "That is a wonderful thing. I love gold."

"Bring your daughter to my castle tomorrow," he says. "I want to see this."

The miller goes home. He is happy and afraid at the same time.

"What do you mean, Father?" asks the daughter. "I cannot spin straw into gold! Nobody can!"

"I know," says the miller. He holds his head in his hands. "I have a big mouth. I am a silly old man."

Chapter 2: A Room Full of Straw

The next morning, the miller and his daughter walk to the castle. The girl is very quiet, and her hands are cold.

The king sits on a gold chair. He smiles at the girl, but it is not a kind smile.

"So you are the clever girl," he says. "Come with me."

They go down some stairs and along a dark hall. At the end of the hall, there is a small room.

The king opens the door. The room is full of straw, from the floor to the window. In the middle of the room, there is a spinning wheel and a little chair.

"Here is some straw, and here is a spinning wheel," says the king. "Spin all this straw into gold tonight. I come back in the morning. If there is no gold, you must die."

The king goes out and locks the door. Click!

The girl is alone. She can spin wool, like every girl in her village. But straw into gold? No.

She sits on the little chair and starts to cry.

"Oh, Father," she says. "What can I do? Nobody can do it."

She cries and cries. The moon comes up, and the room is dark and cold.

Then the door opens, and a little man comes into the room. He is not as tall as the chair. He has a long grey beard, a big nose and a funny red hat. His eyes are black and bright.

"Good evening, miller's daughter," he says. "Why do you cry?"

"Oh!" says the girl. "Who are you? How do you get in? The door is shut!"

The little man laughs. "I can go where I want. Now tell me. Why do you cry?"

"I must spin this straw into gold," says the girl. "But I do not know how."

"Hmm," says the little man. He looks at the straw and touches the spinning wheel. "I can do it. But what do you give me?"

The girl thinks. She has nothing. Then she touches her neck. She has a small necklace with blue beads. It is from her mother.

"I can give you my necklace," she says.

The little man takes the necklace and puts it in his pocket. "Good," he says. "Now sleep."

He sits at the spinning wheel. Whirr, whirr, whirr! The wheel goes round three times, and the reel is full of gold thread.

He puts on another reel. Whirr, whirr, whirr! That reel is full of gold too.

The girl watches him, but her eyes are heavy. Soon she is asleep.

All night the little man spins. In the morning, there is no straw in the room. There are only reels and reels of shining gold. And the little man is not there.

Chapter 3: The Second Night

The sun comes up, and the king comes in. He looks at the gold with his mouth open.

"Gold!" he says at last. "So much gold!"

He touches the reels and counts them. One, two, three, ten, twenty, forty. He laughs.

"You are a very clever girl," he says.

The girl says nothing. She looks at the floor. She does not want to tell him about the little man.

The king is happy. But he is a greedy man, and when he has gold, he wants more gold.

That evening, he takes the girl to another room. This room is very big, and it is full of straw from the floor to the ceiling.

"Spin all this straw into gold tonight," says the king. "If you want to live, you must do it."

The king goes out and locks the door. Click!

There is so much straw! The girl sits down on the floor, puts her head on her knees and starts to cry again.

"Oh, where is the little man?" she says. "Maybe he does not come tonight."

The moon comes up. An owl calls outside. Hoo, hoo!

Then the door opens, and the little man comes in with the same red hat and the same black eyes.

"Good evening, miller's daughter," he says. "You cry a lot."

"Oh, it is you!" says the girl. "I am so happy to see you!"

"I know what you want," says the little man. "Then what do you give me this time?"

On the girl's finger, there is a little ring. It is a cheap ring, but she loves it.

"I can give you my ring," she says.

The little man takes the ring and holds it up to the moonlight. He smiles. "Good. That is a pretty ring."

He sits at the spinning wheel. Whirr, whirr, whirr! The straw goes in and the gold comes out.

"How do you do it?" she asks.

"That is my secret," he says.

"And what is your name?"

The little man stops and looks at her with his black eyes.

"That is my secret too," he says. He laughs a strange little laugh and spins again. Whirr, whirr, whirr!

The girl is very sleepy. Soon she is asleep in the corner.

In the morning, the big room is full of gold. It shines in the sun. There is no straw, and there is no little man.

The king comes in and walks round and round the room. "Wonderful," he says. "Wonderful!"

But he does not say thank you. And he does not say, "You can go home now."

Chapter 4: The Third Night

That evening, the king takes the girl to the biggest room in the castle. It is as big as a church, and it is full of straw.

"This is the last time," says the king. "Spin all this straw into gold tonight. If you do it, you can be my wife and the queen."

The girl looks at the king. She does not love him.

The king thinks, "She is only a miller's daughter. But she can make gold. I cannot find a richer wife in all the world."

"And if there is no gold in the morning," he says, "you know what happens."

He goes out and locks the door. Click!

This time the girl does not cry. She is too sleepy to cry. She sits down and waits.

The wind blows outside. Then the door opens and the little man comes in.

"Good evening, miller's daughter," he says. "Here I am again. What do you give me this time?"

The girl looks at her neck. There is no necklace. She looks at her finger. There is no ring.

"I have nothing at all," she says. "I am sorry."

"Nothing?" says the little man. "Then I cannot help you." He turns to the door.

"No! Please do not go!"

The little man stops and turns round. His black eyes shine.

"Well," he says slowly. "Maybe you can give me something later."

"Later? What do you mean?"

"Soon you are the queen, yes? Then one day you have a baby. Give me your first baby."

The girl looks at him. A baby? Maybe the king does not marry her. Maybe she never has a baby. And she does not want to die tonight.

"Who knows what happens?" she thinks. "Yes," she says. "I promise."

"Good," says the little man. "Remember your promise."

All night he spins. The pile of straw gets smaller and smaller, and the pile of gold gets bigger and bigger.

In the morning, the biggest room in the castle is full of gold. And the little man is not there.

"You are wonderful," he says. "Now you are my wife."

The king and the miller's daughter have a big wedding with music, dancing and a big cake. People come from all over the country.

The miller comes too, in a new coat. He is very proud. "My daughter is the queen!" he says to everyone.

The new queen has a gold crown on her head and a beautiful white dress. She is happy.

But sometimes, at night, she thinks about the little man and her promise. Then she does not feel happy.

"It is only a promise," she says to herself. "Maybe he forgets."

Chapter 5: The Queen and the Baby

A year goes by. The queen is happy in the castle. The king is kinder now, and he does not ask her to spin straw any more. He has enough gold.

The queen has a garden with roses and apple trees, and she walks there every morning.

Then one spring, the queen has a baby boy. He has pink cheeks, soft brown hair and his mother's kind eyes.

The queen loves him very much. She holds him all day and sings to him every night.

"You are my little star," she says. "My little prince."

The bells ring and people dance in the streets. The queen does not think about the little man and her promise any more.

But the little man does not forget.

One evening, the queen is in her room. The baby is in his little bed, and the candles are bright.

Then the door opens, and the little man comes in.

"Good evening, Your Majesty," he says. "Do you remember me? Do you remember your promise?"

The queen stands up. Her face is white. "Yes," she says.

"Then give me the baby. He is mine now."

"No!" says the queen. She stands in front of the little bed. "Please! Not my baby!"

The little man looks at her. He does not move.

"I can give you gold," says the queen. "I have rooms full of gold. Take it all!"

The little man laughs. "Gold? I make gold from straw, remember? I do not want gold."

"I can give you diamonds and horses. I can give you a castle!"

"No," says the little man. "Something alive is better than all the gold in the world. I want the baby."

The queen falls to her knees and cries and cries.

"Please," she says. "He is my little boy. I love him."

The little man watches her for a long time. Then he puts his hand on his beard.

"Well," he says. "I like games. Let us play a game. I give you three days. In three days, you must find my name. If you know my name, you can keep your baby."

"And if I do not know your name?"

"Then the baby is mine." He smiles a cold smile. "But nobody knows my name. Nobody in the whole world."

He goes out of the door. The queen runs to the little bed and holds her baby. She does not sleep all night.

Chapter 6: Day One and Day Two

The queen sits by the window with a candle. She thinks about names and writes them on a long piece of paper.

Thomas. John. Peter. Michael. Paul. William. Hans. Franz. Otto.

In the morning, she calls a messenger. He is a young man with fast legs and a good horse, and his name is Tom.

"Tom," says the queen, "I need your help. Please ride all over the country and ask everyone about names. Bring me every name you hear. Old names, new names, strange names."

"Yes, Your Majesty," says Tom. He jumps on his horse and rides away.

That evening, the little man comes back. He sits on a chair, and his feet do not touch the floor.

"Well, Your Majesty?" he says. "What is my name?"

The queen reads the names from her paper.

"Is your name Caspar?"

"No."

"Is it Melchior?"

"No."

"Is it Balthazar?"

"No, it is not."

She reads every name on the paper, name after name after name. Every time, the little man shakes his head and smiles.

"No," he says. "That is not my name."

At the end, he jumps off the chair. "One day is gone. Now you have only two days." He laughs and goes out.

The next morning, Tom comes back with a new list.

"Your Majesty," he says, "I go to all the villages near the castle. I hear some very strange names."

That evening, the little man comes back. "Well? What is my name today?"

The queen looks at Tom's list.

"Is your name Ribs-of-Beef?"

The little man laughs. "No!"

"Is it Mutton-Chops?"

"No!"

"Is it Lace-Legs?"

"No, no, no!" He laughs so much that his red hat falls off. He puts it back on.

"Is it Bandy-Legs? Is it Long-Nose? Is it Sheep-Shanks?"

"No. No. And no." He jumps up and down. "Tomorrow is the last day. Tomorrow the baby is mine!"

He goes out. The queen sits down and holds the baby close.

"Oh, my little star," she says. "What can I do? Only one day."

She looks out of the window at the dark night. Far away, there is a forest and a high mountain.

"Tom must try one more time," she says. "He must go very far away."

Chapter 7: The Little House in the Forest

Early the next morning, Tom gets on his horse again.

"Go where nobody goes, Tom," says the queen. "This is the last day."

"I can do it, Your Majesty," says Tom. "I promise."

He rides and rides, past the villages and the farms and the last house in the country. Then there are only trees and rocks.

Tom rides into the forest. The trees are old and tall, and it is dark under them. His horse is very slow now, and Tom is sleepy too.

In the afternoon, he comes to a high mountain. It is a wild and lonely place. Here, the fox and the hare say good night to each other.

"There are no people here, so there are no names here," says Tom. "What can I tell the queen?"

He is very sad, and he wants to go back. But then he sees a little light between the trees.

He gets off his horse and walks very quietly. He hides behind a big tree and looks.

There is a very small house. In front of the house, there is a fire. And around the fire, a little man dances.

He has a long grey beard and a funny red hat. He jumps over the fire, he hops on one leg and he waves his arms. And he sings a song.

"Today I bake my bread,
Tomorrow I make my tea,
And then I take the queen's baby,
To live here with me!
Nobody knows my name,
Nobody knows my game,
Ha ha! Rumpelstiltskin
Is my name!"

Tom does not move. He does not breathe.

"Rumpelstiltskin," he says very quietly. "Rumpelstiltskin."

The little man dances and sings his song again and again.

Tom walks slowly back to his horse without a sound. Then he jumps on the horse and rides away very fast. He rides through the dark forest and past the farms and the villages.

"Rumpelstiltskin," he says to himself. "Rumpelstiltskin. I must not forget it."

The sun goes down and the moon comes up. At last he sees the castle. He runs up the stairs to the queen's room.

"Your Majesty!" he says. "I have it! I have his name!"

"Tell me!" says the queen. "Quick! He comes soon!"

Tom tells her about the forest and the mountain, the little house and the fire, the dance and the song. And then he tells her the name.

The queen smiles. It is her first smile in three days.

"Thank you, Tom," she says. "Thank you, thank you."

Chapter 8: What Is My Name?

The queen sits on her chair. The baby is asleep in his little bed. Tom hides behind the curtain because he wants to see.

The door opens and the little man comes in. He is very happy, and he rubs his hands.

"Good evening, Your Majesty," he says. "This is the last day. Do you know my name?"

The queen looks at him with a sad face. But inside, she smiles.

"Hmm," she says. "Is your name Conrad?"

"No!" says the little man.

"Is your name Harry?"

"No!" He laughs and jumps up and down. "No, no, no!"

"Is your name Charles?"

"No! You never know my name. Give me the baby!"

He walks to the little bed and holds out his small hands.

"Wait," says the queen. "One more name."

The little man stops. "One more? Very well. Then the baby is mine."

The queen stands up and looks into his black eyes.

"Is your name," she says slowly, "Rumpelstiltskin?"

The little man does not move. His face is white, then red, then purple.

"Who tells you?" he shouts. "Who tells you my name? Nobody knows it! Nobody!"

"I know it," says the queen. "Rumpelstiltskin."

"No! No! NO!"

Rumpelstiltskin is so angry. He stamps his right foot so hard that his foot goes into the floor. His leg goes in too, and now he cannot move.

He pulls and pulls his leg with both hands. His red hat falls off and his face is purple. At last, his leg comes out.

Then he runs out of the door, down the stairs and out of the castle.

He runs into the dark forest, to the high mountain where the fox and the hare say good night. And he never comes back. Never, ever.

The queen picks up her baby and holds him close. She laughs and cries at the same time.

"My little star," she says. "You are safe now. You can stay with me."

Tom comes out from behind the curtain. "Well done, Your Majesty," he says.

"No, Tom," says the queen. "Well done, you! You are the hero."

The king comes in and sees the hole in the floor. "What is this?" he asks.

The queen tells him everything. She tells him about the straw and the little man, the necklace, the ring and the promise. She tells him about the name.

The king is quiet for a long time. Then he takes her hand.

"I am sorry," he says. "I love gold too much. Now I know. You and the baby are better than all the gold in the world."

The next day, the miller comes to the castle and hears the story too. His face goes red.

"It is all my fault," he says. "I have a big mouth."

"Yes, Father," says the queen, and she smiles. "You do. But please keep it shut now."

Everybody laughs, and the baby laughs too.

From that day, the king is a good king and the queen is a good queen. Tom has a new house and a new horse. And the miller never tells silly stories again.

Well, only sometimes.

The End
