Chapter 1: Jam and Flies

It is a sunny morning in a small town. A little tailor lives in this town. He is small and thin, but he has a very big heart.

The tailor sits on his table by the window. He makes clothes all day. Today he makes a blue jacket for the mayor. The tailor works with his needle and thread. In and out, in and out goes the needle.

"I am a good tailor," he says. "My jackets are the best in town."

A woman walks down the street. She has a big basket. "Good jam!" she calls. "Sweet jam for sale!"

The tailor opens his window. "Here, good woman!" he says. "I want some jam."

The woman comes up the stairs. She opens her basket. There are many pots of jam inside. The tailor smells the red jam, the yellow jam and the purple jam.

"Hmm," he says. "This jam smells very good. I want a little. Just one spoon, please."

The woman is not happy. Her basket is heavy, and the tailor wants only one spoon! She gives him a little jam. Then she goes away. She is angry, but the tailor does not see it.

The tailor has a big piece of bread. He puts the jam on the bread. It looks very good.

"First I finish my jacket," he says. "Then I eat my bread."

He puts the bread on the table and takes his needle again. He sews and sings a happy song.

But the room is warm. The jam is sweet. And many flies live in the room. The flies smell the jam. One fly comes. Then two flies come. Then many flies come. They sit on the bread. They eat his jam!

"Hey!" says the tailor. "Who invites you? This is my jam! Go away!"

The flies do not understand. They do not go away. More flies come to the bread.

Now the tailor is very angry. He takes a piece of cloth. He hits the bread with the cloth. Bang!

He looks at the table. He counts the flies. One, two, three, four, five, six, seven! Seven dead flies lie on the table.

The tailor opens his eyes wide. "Seven!" he says. "Seven with one blow! I am a strong man. I am a very brave man. The town must know this!"

He thinks for a moment. Then he smiles.

"No," he says. "The town is too small. The whole world must know this!"

Chapter 2: The Belt

The tailor forgets the jacket for the mayor. He takes a piece of cloth. He cuts a long belt. Then he takes his needle and some gold thread. He sews big gold letters on the belt. The letters say: "SEVEN AT ONE BLOW."

He puts the belt around his body. He looks at himself in the mirror. He is very proud.

"Look at me!" he says. "I am a hero. A hero cannot work in a little room. A hero must see the world."

There is not much food in his little house. He finds an old piece of cheese. It is soft and white. He puts the cheese in his pocket.

"Good," he says. "Now I have food for my trip."

He opens the door. In the garden, a little bird sits in a bush. Its foot is stuck in the branches. The tailor helps the bird. Then he puts it in his other pocket.

"You can come with me, little friend," he says.

He closes the door of his house and does not look back.

The tailor walks out of the town. He is small, but he walks fast. He walks past green fields, brown cows and white sheep.

"Seven at one blow," he sings. "Seven at one blow! Who is as brave as me?"

After many hours, he comes to a mountain. The road goes up and up. The tailor climbs and climbs. He is tired now, but he does not stop.

At the top of the mountain, he sees something very big. Is it a rock? Is it a house? No. It is a man. But it is not a normal man. It is a giant!

The giant sits on the grass. He is as tall as a tree. His hands are as big as tables.

Many people run away from giants. But not our tailor. He walks right up to the giant.

"Good day, friend!" he says. "What a beautiful day! Do you want to come with me? I want to see the world."

The giant looks down at the tailor. Then he laughs a very loud laugh.

"You?" says the giant. "You are a tiny little man! You are like a mouse! Why do I want to go with you?"

The tailor opens his jacket. He shows the giant his belt.

"Read this," he says. "Then you know what kind of man I am."

The giant reads the gold letters. "Seven at one blow," he reads slowly.

The giant does not know about the flies. He thinks of men. Seven men with one blow! He looks at the little tailor again. Now he does not laugh.

Chapter 3: The Giant's Tests

The giant thinks. "This little man is strong," he says to himself. "But is he stronger than me? I want to know."

He takes a stone from the ground. It is a big, hard stone. He holds it in his hand. Then he squeezes the stone. He squeezes very hard. A little water comes out of the stone. Drip, drip, drip.

"Look," says the giant. "I can squeeze water from a stone. Can you do this, little man?"

"Is that all?" says the tailor. "That is easy for me."

He puts his hand in his pocket. He takes out the soft cheese. The giant does not see well. He thinks the cheese is a stone. The tailor squeezes the cheese. A lot of water runs out of it. It runs down his arm.

"There," says the tailor. "Look at all that water. Is my stone better than your stone?"

The giant does not know what to say. He scratches his head. Then he takes another stone. He throws it up into the air. The stone goes very high. It goes up and up. Then it comes down again, far away.

"Now you, little man," says the giant. "Can you throw a stone so high?"

"Your stone is good," says the tailor. "But it comes down again. My stone does not come down."

He puts his hand in his other pocket. He takes out the little bird. The giant thinks it is a stone. The tailor throws the bird into the air. The bird is free! It is very happy. It flies up and up into the sky. It does not come back.

"Well?" says the tailor. "Where is my stone now?"

The giant looks at the sky. He looks and looks. He cannot see the stone. "You can throw well," he says slowly. "But can you carry something heavy? Let us see."

He takes the little tailor to a big oak tree. The tree lies on the ground. It is very old and very heavy.

"Help me carry this tree," says the giant. "We can take it out of the forest."

"Good," says the tailor. "You take the front. It is light. I take the back, with all the branches. That is the heavy part."

The giant puts the front of the tree on his shoulder. He cannot see behind him. The tailor jumps up into the branches. He sits there and smiles. The giant carries the whole tree. And he carries the little tailor too!

The tailor whistles a song in the branches. The giant walks and walks. The tree is very heavy. The giant is very tired. He breathes hard.

After a long time, the giant cannot walk anymore. "Stop!" he says. "I must put the tree down."

The tailor jumps down from the branches quickly. He holds the tree with his arms.

"Are you tired already?" he says. "You are so big. But you cannot carry a tree!"

The giant is not happy. He feels a little stupid.

Chapter 4: The Night in the Cave

They walk on together. They come to a cherry tree. The tree is full of red cherries. The best cherries are at the top. The giant takes the top of the tree. He bends it down for the tailor.

"Here," he says. "Hold this and eat some cherries."

The tailor holds the top of the tree. But he is too light. The tree goes back up. Whoosh! And the little tailor flies up with it! He flies over the tree. He lands on the other side.

The giant laughs. "What is this?" he says. "You cannot hold a little tree?"

"Oh, I can hold it," says the tailor. "But I see some hunters in the forest. They want to shoot. So I jump over the tree. Can you jump over this tree?"

The giant tries. He jumps, but he cannot do it. His big body gets stuck in the branches. Again the little tailor wins.

"Little man," says the giant, "you are very brave. Come to our cave tonight. You can sleep there with us."

The tailor goes with him. They come to a big cave. Inside, there is a fire. Other giants sit around the fire. Each giant has a whole roasted sheep in his hand. They eat and eat.

The giant shows him a bed. It is a giant bed. It is very big and very long. "Here," says the giant. "Sleep in this bed."

The tailor lies down in the bed. But the bed is too big for him. He feels lost in it. So he gets out. He finds a little corner of the cave. He lies down there and goes to sleep.

At midnight, the giant gets up. He takes a big iron bar. He goes to the bed very quietly. He thinks the tailor is in the bed.

"Now we have no more problems," he says. "This little man is too strong. He is dangerous."

He hits the bed with the iron bar. Bang! Bang! The bed breaks in two. The giant goes back to his place. He is happy now. "The little man is dead," he thinks.

In the morning, the giants go into the forest. They talk and laugh. They do not think about the tailor anymore.

But then they see something. A little man walks out of the forest. He sings a happy song. He looks very well. It is the tailor!

The giants are very afraid. "He is alive!" they shout. "The iron bar does not hurt him! Run! Run, or he kills us all!"

The giants run away very fast. They run over the mountains. They do not come back.

The tailor laughs. Then he walks on.

Chapter 5: The King's Palace

The tailor walks for many days. He follows the road. One day he comes to the garden of a big palace. The grass is soft and the sun is warm. The tailor is very tired. He lies down on the grass. Soon he is asleep.

People from the palace walk in the garden. They see the little man on the grass. They see his belt. They read the gold letters: "SEVEN AT ONE BLOW."

"Oh!" they say. "Who is this man? He is a great hero. Seven at one blow! Why is he here? We have peace now. Is there a war?"

They run to the king. "Your Majesty," they say. "There is a great hero in your garden. He kills seven with one blow! He is a very important man. You must not let him go away."

The king is happy to hear this. "A great hero!" he says. "I want him in my army."

He sends a man to the garden. The man waits next to the tailor. At last the tailor opens his eyes. He stretches his arms.

"Sir," says the man, "the king wants you in his army. Do you want to be a soldier for our king?"

"Yes," says the tailor. "That is why I am here. I am ready to work for the king."

The king gives the tailor a nice house. He gives him good food and fine clothes. Now he does not sew jackets. He eats and sleeps and walks in the garden.

But the soldiers of the king are not happy. They talk together in secret.

"What happens if he gets angry?" they say. "He kills seven with one blow. We cannot fight him. We must leave the army."

They go to the king. "Your Majesty," they say. "We cannot be in the army with this man. He is too dangerous. Please send him away, or we go home."

Now the king is sad. "I do not want to lose all my good soldiers," he thinks. "But I am afraid of this man too. If I send him away, maybe he kills me. Maybe he takes my kingdom."

The king thinks and thinks. Then he has an idea.

Chapter 6: Two Giants

The king calls the tailor to him.

"You are a great hero," says the king. "So I have a job for you. In my forest live two giants. They are very bad. They steal. They burn houses. They hurt people. Nobody can stop them. Please kill these two giants. Then you can marry my daughter. And you get half of my kingdom."

The tailor thinks about it. A princess! Half of a kingdom! A little tailor does not get this every day.

"Yes," he says. "I can do it. Giants are easy for me. I kill seven with one blow. Two giants are nothing."

The king gives him one hundred soldiers. They ride with him to the forest. At the edge of the forest, the tailor stops.

"Wait here," he says to the soldiers. "I can do this alone."

The tailor walks quietly into the forest. Soon he finds the two giants. They are asleep under a big tree. They snore loudly. The branches of the tree move up and down.

The tailor fills his pockets with stones. Then he climbs up into the tree. He sits on a branch above the giants. He takes a stone. He throws it on the chest of the first giant.

The first giant opens his eyes. He pushes the second giant. "Why do you hit me?" he says.

"You are dreaming," says the second giant. "I do not hit you."

They go back to sleep. The tailor takes another stone. This time he throws it at the second giant.

The second giant wakes up. "Hey!" he says. "Why do you throw things at me?"

"I do not throw things," says the first giant. He is angry too.

They argue for a moment. Then they close their eyes again. The tailor takes his biggest stone. He throws it very hard at the first giant. It hits his nose.

"That is too much!" shouts the first giant. He jumps up. He pushes the second giant against the tree. The whole tree shakes. The tailor holds on to his branch.

The second giant gets very angry too. The two giants fight. They pull up trees. They hit each other with the trees. They fight and fight. At last, both giants fall down. They do not get up again.

The tailor jumps down from the tree. He takes out his sword. He hits each giant a few times. Then he walks out of the forest.

"The work is done," he tells the soldiers. "The two giants are dead."

The soldiers go into the forest. They see the giants on the ground. They see the broken trees. They are very afraid of the tailor now.

Chapter 7: The Unicorn and the Wild Pig

The tailor goes back to the king. "The giants are dead," he says. "Now give me your daughter and half of your kingdom."

But the king does not want to give these things to him. "Not so fast," he says. "There is one more job. In my forest lives a unicorn. It is very wild. It has a long, sharp horn. It hurts people. Catch the unicorn first. Then you can marry my daughter."

"A unicorn?" says the tailor. "That is easier than two giants. Seven at one blow, remember?"

He takes a rope and an axe. He goes into the forest alone. Soon the unicorn sees him. It puts its head down. It runs at the tailor very fast. Its horn is long and sharp.

The tailor does not run. He stands in front of a big tree. He waits. The unicorn comes closer and closer. At the last moment, the tailor jumps to the side.

The unicorn cannot stop. It runs into the tree. Its horn goes deep into the wood. The unicorn pulls and pulls. But it cannot get its horn out. It is stuck!

"Now I have you!" says the tailor. He puts the rope around the unicorn's neck. Then he cuts the horn out of the tree with his axe. He takes the unicorn to the king.

The king looks at the unicorn. He is surprised. But he still does not want to give the tailor his daughter.

"One more job," says the king. "Only one. There is a wild pig in my forest. It is very big and very angry. It has long teeth. Catch it, and then you can marry my daughter."

"A pig?" says the tailor. "That is nothing for me."

He goes into the forest again. The wild pig sees him. It runs at him with its long teeth. Its eyes are red. Its mouth is open.

Near the tailor, there is a little church. The tailor runs into the church. The pig runs in after him. The tailor runs to a small window at the back. He jumps out of the window. He is small and thin, so he can do it. But the pig is too big and too fat. It cannot get out.

The tailor runs around to the front of the church. He closes the door. Now the pig is inside. It cannot get out.

The tailor calls the hunters. "Come and look," he says. "The wild pig is in the church. You can take it now."

The king has no more ideas. He must keep his promise. The tailor marries the princess. There is a big party with music and dancing. The little tailor is now a prince.

Chapter 8: The Tailor Talks in His Sleep

The tailor and the princess live in the palace. The tailor is very happy. But the princess does not know one thing. She does not know that her husband is a tailor.

One night, the tailor talks in his sleep. "Boy," he says, "finish the jacket for the mayor. Sew the buttons on it. Or I hit you with my ruler!"

The princess opens her eyes. "A jacket?" she thinks. "Buttons? A ruler? My husband is not a hero. He is just a tailor!"

In the morning, she goes to her father. "Father," she says, "my husband is only a tailor. He talks about jackets in his sleep. I do not want a tailor for a husband."

The king listens. "Do not cry, my daughter," he says. "Tonight, leave your door open. My servants can come into the room. They can tie him up. Then they can take him far away on a ship."

The princess is happy with this plan. But a young servant hears everything. He likes the tailor very much. He goes to the tailor and tells him the plan.

"Thank you, my friend," says the tailor. "Do not worry. I know what to do."

That night, the tailor goes to bed as usual. The princess thinks he is asleep. She opens the door. But the tailor is not asleep. He only closes his eyes.

The servants come to the door. They walk very quietly. They have ropes in their hands.

Then the tailor speaks in a loud voice. "Boy," he says, "finish the jacket! Or I hit you with my ruler! I kill seven with one blow. I kill two giants. I catch a unicorn. I catch a wild pig. Why must I be afraid of the men outside my door?"

The servants hear this. They are very afraid. "He knows we are here!" they say. "He can kill us all!"

They drop their ropes. They run away very fast. Nobody wants to touch the tailor ever again.

The princess thinks about it. "Maybe my husband is a tailor," she says. "But he is a very brave tailor."

From that day, nobody tries to hurt the little tailor. When the old king dies, the tailor becomes the new king. He is a good and fair king. He gives warm jackets to all the poor people.

And on his wall, there is a gold belt. It says: "SEVEN AT ONE BLOW."

He never tells anybody about the flies.

The End
