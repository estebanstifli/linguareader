Chapter 1: The Red Hood

There is a small village at the edge of a big forest. In the village, there is a little house. The house has a green door and a red roof.

A girl lives in the little house. She lives there with her mother. The girl is eight years old. She has brown hair and big brown eyes. She is happy and kind. Everybody in the village loves her.

But the girl loves one person very much. She loves her grandmother.

Grandmother lives in the forest. Her house is far from the village. It is under three big oak trees. Grandmother is old, but she is very clever. She makes cakes and jam, and she sews beautiful clothes.

One day, Grandmother makes a present for the girl. It is a hood. A hood is a soft hat with a long coat. The hood is red velvet, and it is very soft.

"This is for you," Grandmother says.

The girl puts on the red hood. She looks in the mirror. She smiles.

"Oh, Grandmother! It is beautiful!" she says. "Thank you! Thank you!"

"It is red like a cherry," says Grandmother. "And you are sweet like a cherry."

The girl laughs. She gives her grandmother a big kiss.

From that day, the girl wears the red hood every day. She does not want to take it off.

"Look!" says the baker. "Here comes Little Red Riding Hood!"

Soon, everybody calls her Little Red Riding Hood. Nobody remembers her real name. Even her mother calls her Little Red Riding Hood.

Little Red Riding Hood likes her new name. She likes it very much.

In the evening, mother and daughter sit by the fire. Mother tells stories. Little Red Riding Hood listens. She loves stories about the forest. Her favourite stories are about the wolves of the forest.

"Are wolves bad?" asks Little Red Riding Hood.

"Some wolves are very bad," says her mother. "They are big and grey. They have sharp teeth. And they are hungry. Always hungry."

"Are there wolves in our forest?"

Her mother looks at the fire. She does not smile.

"Maybe," she says. "Maybe."

Little Red Riding Hood pulls her red hood close. The fire is warm, but she feels a little cold.

Chapter 2: A Basket for Grandmother

One morning, a boy comes to the little house. He is the son of the woodcutter. He knocks on the green door.

"I have a message," he says. "It is from your grandmother. She is ill. She is in bed. She is very weak."

Mother's face is sad. "Oh, no! My poor mother!"

"Is Grandmother very ill?" asks Little Red Riding Hood.

"She is old," says her mother. "Old people get ill. But she needs food. Good food can help her."

Mother goes into the kitchen. She takes eggs and flour and butter. She makes a cake. It is a big round cake with honey on top. The kitchen smells sweet.

Then she takes a bottle from the cupboard. It is a bottle of red wine.

"Wine and cake," says Mother. "They can make your grandmother strong again."

She puts the cake and the wine in a basket. She puts a white cloth on top.

"Little Red Riding Hood," she says. "I must work today. I cannot go to the forest. Can you take this basket to Grandmother?"

Little Red Riding Hood jumps up. "Yes! Yes, I can!"

Mother holds the girl by the shoulders and looks into her eyes. "Now listen to me very carefully," she says.

"The forest is beautiful. But it is big. And it is dark in some places. You must stay on the path. Do you understand? Do not leave the path."

"I must stay on the path," says Little Red Riding Hood.

"Do not talk to strangers," says her mother.

"What is a stranger?"

"A stranger is somebody you do not know. Do not talk to them. Do not tell them where you go."

"I must not talk to strangers," says Little Red Riding Hood.

"Now go," she says with a smile. "Go before the sun is high."

Little Red Riding Hood puts on her red hood and takes the heavy basket.

She walks out of the green door and down the street.

She walks past the market, the well and the last house in the village.

Then she stops. In front of her, there is the forest. The trees are tall and green. The path goes into the trees like a little brown snake.

Little Red Riding Hood takes a deep breath.

"Stay on the path," she says to herself. "Do not run. Do not talk to strangers."

Then she walks into the forest.

Chapter 3: Into the Forest

The forest is very quiet. The trees are very tall. Their leaves are green and gold. The sun comes through the leaves in long yellow lines.

Little Red Riding Hood walks on the path. Her feet make a soft sound on the ground.

"This is nice," she says. "The forest is not scary. The forest is lovely."

She walks and walks. The path goes up a small hill. At the bottom of the hill, there is a stream with clear, cold water and a little wooden bridge.

On the other side of the stream, the trees are bigger and the path is narrow and dark.

Little Red Riding Hood walks on. Soon she hears a loud sound from the trees. Chop, chop, chop!

Near the path, there is a man with an axe. He cuts a big tree. He is the woodcutter, the father of the boy from this morning.

"Good morning!" she says.

"Good morning, Little Red Riding Hood," says the woodcutter. He stops and wipes his face. "You are far from home."

"I go to my grandmother's house. She is ill."

"Ah, yes. My son tells me." He looks at the dark trees. "Be careful, little one. Stay on the path. There are wolves in this forest."

"I know," she says. "My mother tells me."

"Good. Your mother is a clever woman." Then he lifts his axe again.

Little Red Riding Hood walks on. The sound of the axe gets quieter and quieter. Soon she cannot hear it.

Now the forest is very quiet again, and no birds sing.

She holds the basket tight and walks a little faster.

Behind a big tree, two yellow eyes look at her.

Chapter 4: The Wolf

The yellow eyes come closer and closer. Then a wolf steps onto the path.

He is a big grey wolf with a long nose, big ears and a thick tail. His mouth is full of long white teeth.

Little Red Riding Hood stops. She looks at the wolf. The wolf looks at her.

She is not afraid, because she does not know how bad wolves are. To her, he is just a big dog.

The wolf smiles. It is a big smile with many teeth.

"Good morning, Little Red Riding Hood," he says. His voice is soft and sweet, like honey.

"Good morning, Mr Wolf," she says. She is always polite.

"Where do you go so early, little girl?"

She remembers her mother's words. Do not talk to strangers. But the wolf is so polite. And his voice is so sweet.

"I go to my grandmother's house," she says.

"Oh, how nice," says the wolf. "And what is in your basket?"

"Cake and wine. My grandmother is ill. She is weak. The cake and wine can make her strong."

The wolf looks at the basket. He looks at the girl. He licks his lips.

"Very kind," he says. "Very, very kind. And where does your grandmother live, my dear?"

"Deeper in the forest," says Little Red Riding Hood. "It is fifteen minutes from here. Her house is under three big oak trees. There is a hedge of nut trees near the house. You must know it."

"Yes, yes," says the wolf. "I know it."

The wolf thinks very fast.

"This girl is young and soft, and she is a nice meal," he thinks. "But the old woman is a meal, too. I am very hungry, so I want both of them. I must be clever."

He walks next to Little Red Riding Hood, slow and friendly.

"Listen to the birds in the trees, my dear," he says. "They sing so beautifully."

The wolf stops. He looks at her with his yellow eyes.

"But, my dear," he says. "You do not look around you. You look only at the path. Look! Look at the flowers!"

Little Red Riding Hood looks. Between the trees, there are hundreds of red, yellow, blue and white flowers. They are so pretty in the sun.

"Oh!" she says. "How beautiful!"

"Your grandmother loves flowers, yes?" says the wolf.

"Yes! She loves flowers very much!"

"Then take her some flowers," says the wolf. "A big bunch of flowers. She can put them on her table. They can make her happy. Flowers are good for ill people."

Little Red Riding Hood looks at the flowers. She looks at the path.

"But my mother says I must stay on the path."

The wolf laughs softly. "Only for a minute, my dear. The flowers are very near. It is early. You have lots of time."

Little Red Riding Hood thinks about it. Grandmother loves flowers, it is early, and it is only for a minute.

"Yes," she says. "You are right, Mr Wolf. Thank you."

"Not at all, my dear," says the wolf. "Not at all."

He bows to her, and then he walks into the trees. His grey tail is the last thing she sees.

Chapter 5: Flowers off the Path

Little Red Riding Hood steps off the path. The grass is soft and long, and it comes up to her knees.

She picks a red flower that smells sweet.

"One for Grandmother," she says.

Then she sees a yellow flower, a little further away and prettier than the red one.

"Two for Grandmother," she says.

She picks the yellow flower. Then she sees a blue flower, a little further away and prettier than the yellow one.

"Three for Grandmother," she says.

And so it goes. Every time she picks a flower, she sees a prettier one. And every prettier flower is a little further away. She walks and walks, and she picks and picks.

Soon she has a big bunch of flowers. They are red and yellow and blue and white. She puts them in the basket, next to the cake.

"Why are you angry, little squirrel?" she asks.

The squirrel does not answer. It runs up the tree. Chit! Chit!

Little Red Riding Hood looks around. She cannot see the path, the stream or the woodcutter. There are only trees and flowers and more trees.

"Where is the path?" she asks.

Her heart beats fast. "Oh, no. Where am I?"

Then she hears the soft sound of the stream, and she walks to it. And there, near the water, is the path. The little brown path.

"Oh, good," she says happily. "There you are, path!"

She steps back onto the path. She looks up, and the sun is high in the sky now. It is not early any more.

"Oh, no!" she says. "Grandmother is waiting! Poor Grandmother!"

She walks very fast, but she does not run, because of the bottle of wine in the basket.

But where is the wolf?

The wolf does not pick flowers. The wolf does not sing songs. The wolf does not stop at streams.

The wolf runs on the shortest way through the dark trees. He jumps over logs and runs past the three big oak trees.

And he stops at a little house with a hedge of nut trees.

It is Grandmother's house.

Chapter 6: At Grandmother's House

Grandmother's house is small and pretty, with white walls and roses by the door.

The wolf goes to the door and knocks three times.

Inside the house, Grandmother is in bed, too weak to get up.

"Who is there?" she asks.

The wolf makes his voice soft and high. "It is me, Grandmother. Little Red Riding Hood. I have cake and wine for you. Open the door, please."

Grandmother smiles. "Oh, my dear child! I am too weak to get up. Just lift the latch. The door is open."

The wolf lifts the latch. The door opens. He walks into the house.

Grandmother looks up and sees the wolf with his yellow eyes and big teeth.

"Oh!" she says. "You are not Little Red Riding Hood!"

The wolf does not say a word. He jumps to the bed, opens his big mouth and eats Grandmother in one bite.

"Mmm, very nice," says the wolf, and he licks his lips. "But I am still hungry. Now I want the girl, too."

He looks around the room. On a chair, there are Grandmother's nightdress, her white nightcap and her glasses.

The wolf smiles. "Perfect," he says.

He puts on the nightdress, but it is too small. He puts the nightcap over his ears and the glasses on his long nose.

Then he gets into Grandmother's bed and pulls the blanket up to his nose. He closes the curtains, and now the room is dark.

The wolf waits and waits.

Outside, Little Red Riding Hood comes along the path. She sees the three big oak trees, the hedge of nut trees and the little white house.

"Here I am, Grandmother!" she says.

But then she stops, because the door of the house is open.

"That is strange," she thinks. "Grandmother's door is never open."

She walks to the door and looks inside. It is dark and very quiet.

She feels strange and a little afraid. Usually she is so happy at Grandmother's house. But today, something is not right.

"Good morning, Grandmother!" she calls.

There is no answer.

She walks inside and puts the basket on the table.

She walks to the bed and opens the curtains a little.

And there is Grandmother in her bed. Her nightcap is low over her face, and the blanket is up to her chin.

But Grandmother looks very, very strange.

Chapter 7: What Big Teeth You Have

Little Red Riding Hood stands next to the bed and looks at Grandmother.

"Oh, Grandmother," she says. "What big ears you have!"

"All the better to hear you with, my dear," says the wolf in his soft, old voice.

Little Red Riding Hood looks closer.

"Oh, Grandmother," she says. "What big eyes you have!"

"All the better to see you with, my dear."

Little Red Riding Hood looks at the blanket. Something grey comes out from under it.

"Oh, Grandmother," she says. "What big hands you have!"

"All the better to hold you with, my dear."

Little Red Riding Hood looks at the mouth, and it is very, very big.

"But Grandmother," she says. Her voice is very small. "What a terrible big mouth you have!"

"All the better to eat you with!"

The wolf jumps out of the bed, and the glasses fall on the floor. He opens his big mouth and eats Little Red Riding Hood in one bite.

Now the wolf is very full, and his stomach is as big and round as a barrel.

"Oh," he says. "What a good meal. A grandmother and a girl. Now I am tired."

He lies down in Grandmother's bed again, and soon he is asleep.

And then he snores so loudly that the windows shake.

Outside, in the forest, a hunter walks on the path. He is a tall man with a green hat and a gun on his back. He knows the old woman in the little house, and he likes her.

The hunter hears the snoring and stops.

"What is that noise?" he says. "It comes from the old woman's house."

"The old woman never snores like that," he says. "She is ill, so maybe she needs help."

He walks to the house, goes in through the open door and walks to the bed.

And there, in Grandmother's bed, is the wolf.

The hunter's eyes are wide. "You!" he says. "I know you, you old sinner! I look for you every day! And here you are! Now I have you!"

He takes the gun from his back, but then he looks at the wolf's big round stomach.

"Wait," he thinks. "Maybe the old woman is inside the wolf. Maybe I can still save her."

He puts down the gun and does not shoot.

Instead, he takes out a pair of big scissors. The wolf snores and snores, and he does not wake up.

The hunter starts to cut the wolf's stomach open. Snip, snip! He sees something red, and he cuts again.

Then out jumps a little girl in a red hood!

Chapter 8: The Wolf and the Stones

"Oh!" says Little Red Riding Hood. "Thank you! Thank you! It is so dark inside the wolf! I am so afraid!"

The hunter cuts again, and out comes Grandmother. She is very old and very weak, but she is alive.

"Oh, my dear child," says Grandmother. She hugs Little Red Riding Hood. "You are safe. We are both safe."

Little Red Riding Hood looks at the sleeping wolf. Then she looks at the hunter.

"I have an idea," she says. "Wait here."

She runs outside. In the garden next to the house, there are many big stones. She picks up one heavy stone, then another, then another.

The hunter helps her. Together, they bring the stones inside and put them in the wolf's stomach.

Then Grandmother takes a needle and thread. She sews the wolf's stomach, in and out, in and out, with her clever old hands.

Then they hide behind the door and wait.

At last, the wolf wakes up and sees the hunter's green hat behind the door. He wants to run away, so he jumps out of bed.

But the stones are so heavy! He cannot run, and he falls down on the floor. He cannot get up, and there he dies.

The hunter takes the wolf's grey coat. He puts it over his shoulder. "This wolf never hurts anybody again," he says.

Grandmother eats the cake and drinks the wine. Her face is pink again, and she feels much stronger.

"This is the best cake in the world," she says.

Little Red Riding Hood sits on the bed next to Grandmother and holds her hand.

"Grandmother," she says quietly. "I talk to the wolf on the path, and I tell him where you live. Then I leave the path for the flowers. It is all my fault."

Grandmother strokes her hair. "You are young, my dear. Now you know. That is the important thing."

Little Red Riding Hood nods. She says to herself, "Never again. I never leave the path, and I never talk to strangers. My mother is right."

The hunter walks her home. Her mother waits at the green door. She runs to her daughter and hugs her tight.

Some weeks later, Little Red Riding Hood takes another basket to Grandmother. On the path, another wolf comes. He is big and grey, too, and he speaks in a sweet voice.

"Good morning, little girl. Where do you go?"

But Little Red Riding Hood does not answer. She walks fast, straight to Grandmother's house.

"Grandmother!" she says. "There is a wolf on the path! He has a sweet voice and bad eyes!"

Grandmother locks the door. Soon the wolf knocks, but they do not open the door. They are quiet like mice.

The wolf climbs onto the roof. He wants to wait there for the girl.

But Grandmother is clever. "Child," she says. "Bring me the bucket. Today I cook sausages."

There is a big stone trough in front of the house. Little Red Riding Hood carries the water from the sausages to the trough. She carries bucket after bucket, and soon the trough is full.

The smell of sausages goes up to the roof. The wolf sniffs, and he leans down more and more. Then he slips. He falls from the roof into the big trough. Splash! He cannot swim. And that is the end of the second wolf.

Little Red Riding Hood walks home in her red hood. She stays on the path and sings with the birds. And no wolf ever comes near her again.

The End
