Chapter 1: The Garden Next Door

A man and his wife live in a small old house. They are poor, but they are happy.

But they have no children. Every day the wife says, "I want a baby. I want a little girl or a little boy."

"One day, my love," says the man.

The years go by. Then one winter, the wife has a secret. She is going to have a baby!

At the back of the house, there is a small window. The wife sits there every day and looks out at the garden of the next house.

A high grey wall goes round this garden. Inside, there are flowers, apple trees and carrots. Everything is green and fresh.

But nobody goes into this garden. It is the home of an enchantress. An enchantress is a woman with magic. Her name is Dame Gothel, and everybody is afraid of her.

"Do not look at that garden," says the man. "It is dangerous."

But the wife looks and looks. One day, she sees a little bed of green plants with long leaves and small blue flowers. The plants are rapunzel. People eat the leaves in a salad.

"Oh," says the wife. "Rapunzel! I love rapunzel. It is so green and so fresh."

She looks at the rapunzel all morning and all afternoon. In the evening, she does not eat her soup.

The next day, she does not eat her bread or her cheese. She only sits at the window and looks at the rapunzel.

Day after day, she gets thin. Her face is white, and her eyes are sad.

"My dear wife," says the man. "What is wrong? Are you ill?"

"I want the rapunzel from that garden," she says. "I must eat it. I cannot eat anything else."

"But that is the garden of Dame Gothel!" says the man.

"I know," says the wife. "But without it, I am going to die."

The man looks at his thin wife. He loves her very much. He thinks, "I cannot let her die. I must go into the garden."

Chapter 2: Over the Wall

It is night, and the moon is in the sky. The man puts on his dark coat and takes a basket.

"Be careful," says his wife.

"I am always careful," says the man. But his hands shake.

He walks to the wall and looks up. It is very, very high. "Oh dear," he says.

He finds a hole for his foot, then another hole. He climbs and climbs. At the top, he looks down into the dark garden. Nobody is there.

He jumps down into the soft earth and runs to the rapunzel. Quick, quick! He pulls up the plants and puts them in his basket. Then he climbs back over the wall. He is home!

His wife makes a salad with the rapunzel and eats it all. "Mmm," she says. "This is the best salad in the world!"

But the next day, she wants more. "Please," she says. "Only one more time. I want more rapunzel, three times more than before!"

The man sighs. "One more time," he says. "Only one."

The next night, he puts on his dark coat again. He takes the basket, climbs the wall and jumps down into the garden again.

But this time, somebody is there.

A tall woman stands in front of him. She has a long black dress, grey hair and green eyes. Her eyes shine in the dark like the eyes of a cat. It is Dame Gothel!

The man is so afraid that he cannot move or speak.

"So!" says Dame Gothel in a cold voice. "You are the thief! You come into my garden and steal my rapunzel. You are going to be very sorry."

"Please!" says the man, and he falls to his knees. "I am not a bad man. My wife sees your rapunzel from our window. She is going to have a baby. Without your rapunzel, she is going to die!"

Dame Gothel looks at the man for a long time. Then she smiles. It is not a nice smile.

"A baby?" she says. "Very well. Take all the rapunzel you want. Your wife can eat it every day."

"Thank you!" says the man. "Thank you!"

"But when the baby comes, you must give it to me," says Dame Gothel. "I can be a good mother to the child."

The man is so afraid that he cannot think. "Yes, yes," he says. "Anything!"

"Good," says Dame Gothel. "Remember your promise."

The man goes home with a full basket. His wife eats rapunzel every day, and she gets strong again. Her face is pink again.

But the man is not happy. Every night, he thinks about his promise.

Chapter 3: A Girl with Golden Hair

Spring comes, then summer. Then the baby comes. It is a little girl, small and pink and very beautiful.

The wife holds the baby and sings to her. The man smiles and smiles.

But then there is a knock at the door. Knock, knock, knock.

The man opens the door. Dame Gothel stands there in her long black dress.

"I am here for the baby," she says.

"No!" says the wife. "Please! She is our baby!"

"A promise is a promise," says Dame Gothel, and she takes the baby in her arms. "Her name is Rapunzel, like the plant from my garden."

She goes away with the baby. The man and his wife cry and cry. They never see their little girl again.

Rapunzel grows up in the house of Dame Gothel, behind the high wall, in the beautiful garden.

Dame Gothel is not a warm mother. She does not sing songs or tell stories. But she gives Rapunzel good food and nice clothes. She teaches her to read and to sew. Rapunzel calls her "Mother Gothel".

Rapunzel is a happy child. She plays in the garden all day and talks to the birds, the bees and the flowers.

"Good morning, red rose," she says. "Good morning, little bee. How are you today?"

Rapunzel can sing very well. Her voice is sweet and clear, like the voice of a bird. When she sings, the birds stop and listen.

Rapunzel grows and grows. And her hair grows and grows. It is long and golden like the sun, and soft like silk. It is the most beautiful hair in the world.

One day, Rapunzel climbs an apple tree and looks over the high wall. In the window of a little old house, she sees a man and a woman. They look very sad.

"Mother Gothel," says Rapunzel. "Who are the people in that little house?"

"Nobody," says Dame Gothel with a cold face. "Do not look over the wall. The world outside is bad."

When Rapunzel is twelve years old, she is the most beautiful girl in the land. Dame Gothel looks at her and thinks.

"Soon somebody is going to see her and take her away from me," she thinks. "No! She is mine. Only mine."

That night, Dame Gothel walks deep into the forest. There, she lifts her hands and says some magic words.

The ground shakes. Big grey stones come up out of the earth. They go up and up, and soon there is a tall tower.

The tower has no door and no stairs. It has only one small window, at the very top.

Dame Gothel smiles. "Perfect," she says.

Chapter 4: The Tower in the Forest

The next morning, Dame Gothel takes Rapunzel by the hand. "Come," she says. "We are going for a walk."

"Outside the garden?" says Rapunzel. "Oh, Mother Gothel, thank you!"

They go deep into the dark forest. Rapunzel is very happy. She looks at everything.

Then they come to the tower.

"What a tall tower!" says Rapunzel. "Where is the door?"

"There is no door," says Dame Gothel. She holds Rapunzel's hand very tight and says a magic word. And then they are in a small round room at the top of the tower.

"This is your new home," says Dame Gothel. "Here, you are safe. Here, nobody can find you."

"But I want to go home," says Rapunzel. "I want to see my birds and my flowers."

"No," says Dame Gothel. "You stay here. I come every day and bring you food."

And she goes away. Rapunzel sits on her bed and cries. She is all alone.

The next day, Dame Gothel comes back. She stands at the bottom of the tower, looks up at the window and calls:

"Rapunzel, Rapunzel,
Let down your hair!"

Rapunzel goes to the window. Her hair is twenty metres long now! She puts it round a hook at the window and lets it fall down, down, down to the ground.

Dame Gothel takes the golden hair in her hands. And she climbs up, up, up and in at the window.

"Ow!" says Rapunzel. "Mother Gothel, you are heavy!"

"Do not complain," says Dame Gothel. "Here is your bread, your milk and your cheese."

Every day is the same. Every day, Dame Gothel calls, "Rapunzel, Rapunzel, let down your hair!" And every day, she climbs up the golden hair.

The days are long in the tower. Rapunzel reads her books again and again. She sews and combs her hair. She looks out of the window at the trees.

Birds come to the window, a little brown bird and a big black bird. They are her new friends.

"Hello, little friends," says Rapunzel, and she gives them some bread. "Do you want to hear a song?"

She sings about the sun and the sky and the green forest. She sings about the big world outside.

"What is out there?" she asks the birds. "Are there towns? Are there other girls like me? Does anybody hear my song?"

The birds do not answer. They only sing with her.

Year after year, Rapunzel stays in the tower. She is fourteen, then fifteen, then sixteen. And she sings every day.

Chapter 5: The Prince in the Forest

One summer day, a young prince rides through the forest on his white horse. He is the son of the king, and he likes the quiet of the trees.

Then the prince hears a girl singing. Her voice is sweet and clear, like the voice of a bird.

"What a beautiful song!" he says. "Who is singing? I must find her."

He rides towards the voice through the thick trees. Then he comes to a tall grey tower. The song comes from the small window at the top.

"Hello!" calls the prince. But the girl cannot hear him, and the song does not stop.

The prince rides round the tower. There is no door. There are no stairs. There is no way in!

"How strange," he says. "How does the girl get in and out?"

The prince goes home to his castle, but he cannot forget the song. At dinner he does not eat, and at night he does not sleep.

The next day, he rides back to the tower. He sits under a tree and listens to the song. The day after, he comes again. Every day, he comes to the tower and listens.

One day, he hears somebody in the trees. He hides behind a big oak tree. A tall woman in a long black dress comes to the bottom of the tower. She looks up and calls:

"Rapunzel, Rapunzel,
Let down your hair!"

A great rope of golden hair falls from the window to the ground. The woman takes the hair and climbs up, up, up.

The prince's mouth is open. "So that is the way in!" he says quietly. "It is not a rope. It is hair! And her name is Rapunzel."

After some time, the woman climbs down the golden hair and walks away into the forest.

The prince waits until the sun goes down. The sky is red, then purple, then dark.

"Now it is my turn," he says.

He goes to the bottom of the tower. His heart beats fast. He looks up at the window and calls:

"Rapunzel, Rapunzel,
Let down your hair!"

Chapter 6: A Stranger at the Window

The golden hair falls down. The prince takes it in his hands and climbs up, up, up and in at the window.

Rapunzel turns round and screams. "Aaah! Who are you? You are not Mother Gothel!"

Rapunzel does not know any men. She only knows Dame Gothel. She takes a big book from the table and holds it up high.

"Stop!" she says. "Do not move!"

"Please do not be afraid," says the prince, and he holds up his hands. "I am a friend."

"A friend?" says Rapunzel. She does not put down the book.

"Yes," says the prince. "Every day I sit under that tree and listen to your song. Your voice is so beautiful. I cannot eat or sleep. I must meet you."

Rapunzel looks at him. He has kind eyes and a nice smile. Slowly, she puts down the book.

"You hear my song every day?" she says. "So somebody does hear me!" And she smiles.

They sit down by the window and talk and talk. The prince tells her about the world outside. He tells her about towns and markets and the sea.

"What is the sea?" says Rapunzel.

"It is water, water, water," says the prince. "Blue water, as far as you can see."

"Oh," says Rapunzel. "I want to see it. I want to see everything."

When the moon comes up, the prince must go. "Can I come back tomorrow?" he asks.

"Yes," says Rapunzel. "Please come back."

So every evening, the prince waits for Dame Gothel to go. Then he calls, "Rapunzel, Rapunzel, let down your hair!"

Rapunzel and the prince talk and laugh and read books together. She sings for him, and he sings for her. But he does not sing very well.

"Your song is like a bird," he says. "My song is like a frog."

Rapunzel laughs and laughs.

One evening, the prince takes her hand. "Rapunzel," he says. "I love you. Do you want to marry me and come with me to my castle?"

Rapunzel thinks, "Mother Gothel is old and cold. He is young and kind. And I love him."

"Yes!" she says. "But how can I get out? I cannot climb down my own hair."

Then Rapunzel has an idea. "Bring me a piece of silk every evening," she says. "I can make a silk ladder. When the ladder is long, I can climb down. Then you can take me away on your horse."

"A ladder!" says the prince. "You are very clever."

So every evening, the prince brings a piece of silk, and Rapunzel makes the ladder longer. She hides it under her bed. Dame Gothel comes in the day, and the prince comes at night. And Dame Gothel does not know anything.

Chapter 7: Scissors

One morning, Dame Gothel calls, "Rapunzel, Rapunzel, let down your hair!"

Rapunzel lets down her golden hair, and Dame Gothel climbs up. She is very slow and heavy today.

Rapunzel is tired. She thinks of the prince, and she does not think about her words.

"Ow, ow!" she says. "Mother Gothel, why are you so heavy and slow? The prince is not heavy. He climbs up in one minute!"

Then she puts her hand over her mouth. Oh no!

Dame Gothel's face goes white, then red. Her green eyes shine like fire.

"The prince?" she says in a very quiet voice. "What prince?"

Rapunzel says nothing.

"Oh, you bad, bad girl!" says Dame Gothel. "I hide you from the world. I give you everything. And you lie to me!"

She finds the silk ladder under the bed. "And what is this?" she screams.

Dame Gothel takes Rapunzel's long golden hair and puts it round her hand. Then she takes a big pair of scissors from her bag.

Snip! Snap!

The golden hair falls on the floor like a big golden snake. Now Rapunzel's hair is very short.

"My hair!" cries Rapunzel. "My beautiful hair!"

But Dame Gothel takes Rapunzel by the arm and says a magic word. Now they are not in the tower. They are in a desert.

A desert is a place with no water. There are no trees and no flowers, only sand and rocks. The sun is hot in the day, and the wind is cold at night.

"You stay here," says Dame Gothel. "Here, nobody can find you. Goodbye, Rapunzel."

Then she is not there any more. Rapunzel sits on a rock all alone, and she cries and cries.

Dame Gothel goes back to the tower. She puts the long golden hair round the hook at the window. Then she sits and waits.

When the sun goes down, the prince comes. He is happy, and he has a big piece of silk for the ladder. He stands at the bottom of the tower and calls:

"Rapunzel, Rapunzel,
Let down your hair!"

The golden hair falls down, and the prince climbs up and in at the window.

But Rapunzel is not there. Dame Gothel is there! Her green eyes shine in the dark like the eyes of a cat.

"Aha!" she says. "You want your dear Rapunzel? Your pretty bird is not in her nest now. The cat has her! And the cat wants your eyes too. You never see Rapunzel again! Never!"

The prince is very sad. He does not want to live without Rapunzel. He goes to the window, and he jumps.

He falls down, down, down. He does not die. He lands in a big bush of thorns. Thorns are sharp, like needles, and they go into his eyes.

The prince gets up and opens his eyes. But everything is dark. He cannot see. He is blind.

Chapter 8: Tears

The prince walks into the forest with his hands in front of him. He cannot see the trees or the path.

He walks and walks. He has no horse and no home. He eats roots and berries, and he drinks water from the streams. At night, he sleeps under the trees.

"Rapunzel," he says every day. "Where are you, Rapunzel?"

Weeks go by, then months, then years. And the prince walks and walks.

Far away in the desert, Rapunzel lives in a little hut of rocks and sticks. She finds a little water under the rocks, and she eats wild plants.

She is not alone now. She has twins, a boy and a girl. The boy has dark eyes like his father. The girl has golden hair like her mother.

Every day, Rapunzel sings to her children. She sings about the forest, the tower and the prince.

"Where is your father?" she says to them. "Is he alive? Does he think of us?"

One day, the prince comes to the end of the forest. He feels hot sand under his feet and the hot sun on his face.

"A desert," he says. "There is nothing here for me."

He sits down on a rock. He is very tired and very hungry.

Then he hears a woman singing. Her voice is sweet and clear, like the voice of a bird.

The prince stands up. His heart beats fast. "I know that voice," he says. "I know that song!"

He walks towards the voice. He falls down, gets up and walks again.

Rapunzel is in front of her hut with the children. She sees a thin man in an old coat. He walks with his hands in front of him.

Then she sees his face. "It is him!" she says. "It is my prince!"

She runs to him and puts her arms round him. "My love, my love!" she says. "It is me! It is Rapunzel!"

"Rapunzel!" says the prince. "But I cannot see you. My eyes are dark."

Rapunzel looks at his eyes and cries. Two tears fall from her eyes into his eyes.

And then something wonderful happens. The prince blinks and blinks. He sees light! He sees the blue sky! He sees Rapunzel's face!

"I can see!" he says. "Rapunzel, I can see you!"

They laugh and cry and hold each other for a long, long time.

Then Rapunzel takes his hand. "Come," she says. "These are your children."

The prince looks at the little boy and the little girl. "Oh, Rapunzel!" he says. "Today is the best day of my life."

The prince takes Rapunzel and the children home to his kingdom. It is a long walk, but nobody minds. There is music and dancing for a week.

The prince and Rapunzel get married and live in a big castle with a big garden. And in the garden, there is a little bed of green plants with blue flowers.

Rapunzel's hair grows long again. But there are no towers in her life now. There are doors and stairs, and her window is always open.

And Dame Gothel? She lives alone in her tower in the forest. She calls and calls, but nobody lets down any hair for her.

The End
