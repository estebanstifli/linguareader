Chapter 1: A Quiet Little House

There is a little house at the end of a long road. It has a red door and a green garden. A little old woman and a little old man live in the house.

The old woman has white hair and a blue apron. She likes flowers, tea and cake. The old man has a long grey beard. He likes his garden, his chair and his newspaper.

The house is very quiet. There are no children in the house. There is only a fat old cat. Her name is Tibby, and she sleeps all day by the fire.

Every morning the old woman looks out of the window. Children walk to school on the road. They laugh and sing, and they run and jump.

"I love children," says the old woman. "They are so happy, and they are so fast."

"Yes," says the old man. "But we are old, and we have no children."

The old woman thinks for a minute. Then she smiles a big smile.

"I have an idea," she says. "I can make a little boy."

The old man laughs. "A little boy? How?"

"With gingerbread," she says.

Gingerbread is a sweet brown cake with ginger and honey in it. The old woman makes very good gingerbread, and everyone in the village knows it.

She goes into the kitchen and takes a big bowl. She puts flour, sugar, butter and an egg in the bowl. She puts in honey and ginger too. Then she mixes and mixes.

The old man watches her. "Can I help?" he asks.

"Yes," she says. "You can taste it."

He tastes it. "Mmm, very good," he says. "Very, very good."

"Only one taste," she says.

She puts the soft brown dough on the table and cuts a little man. He has a round head, two arms and two legs.

"He needs a face," says the old man.

"Yes, he does," says the old woman. She takes two raisins. "These are his eyes." She takes a red cherry. "This is his nose." She takes a long orange sweet. "And this is his mouth."

"He needs a coat too," says the old man.

The old woman takes three little white sweets and puts them in a line. They are the buttons on his coat. Then she puts white sugar on his hands and on his feet.

"Oh, he is beautiful," says the old man.

"He is our little Gingerbread Man," says the old woman.

She opens the oven. It is very hot inside. She puts the Gingerbread Man in the oven and closes the door.

"Now we wait," she says.

They sit at the table and drink their tea. The cat sleeps by the fire. The kitchen smells of ginger and honey, and it is a warm, sweet smell.

Chapter 2: Out of the Oven

The old woman and the old man wait and drink their tea. Then the old woman hears a little sound. Tap, tap, tap.

"What is that?" she asks.

"I don't know," says the old man.

Tap, tap, tap. The sound comes from the oven. The old woman stands up and walks to the oven. She listens.

"Let me out, let me out," says a little voice. "It is very hot in here!"

The old woman looks at the old man, and the old man looks at the old woman. The cat opens one eye.

"The oven can talk?" asks the old man.

"No," says the old woman. "Ovens can't talk."

She opens the oven door, and the Gingerbread Man jumps out onto the table. He is brown and warm. His raisin eyes shine and his orange mouth smiles.

"Hello, and thank you," he says. "That oven is too hot for me."

"Oh my, you can talk," says the old woman.

"And you can walk," says the old man.

"I can walk," says the Gingerbread Man. "And I can run. Look!"

He runs across the table and around the teapot. He runs around the cups and jumps over a spoon.

The old woman claps her hands. "Oh, you are wonderful," she says. "You are our little boy, and you can live here with us."

The old man looks at the Gingerbread Man. He smells the ginger and the honey.

"He smells very good," says the old man. "Maybe I can have one little bite. Only one bite, only his foot."

"No," says the Gingerbread Man. "Nobody eats me, and I need my feet!"

He jumps off the table and lands on the floor. Now the cat opens two eyes. She looks at the Gingerbread Man and licks her lips.

"Oh no," says the Gingerbread Man. "Not you too!"

He runs to the door. The door is open, so he runs out into the garden.

"Stop, stop," says the old woman. "Come back, little Gingerbread Man!"

The Gingerbread Man stops at the gate and turns. He laughs, and then he sings a little song.

"Run, run, as fast as you can, you can't catch me, I'm the Gingerbread Man!"

He runs down the road. The old woman runs after him. The old man runs after the old woman, and the cat runs after the old man.

But the old woman is old and her legs are slow. The old man is old too, and his back hurts. And the cat is very, very fat.

The Gingerbread Man runs and runs on his fast little legs. Soon he is far away.

The old woman stops and sits on a big stone. "Oh dear," she says. "My little boy!"

The old man sits next to her. "Oh dear," he says. "My little bite!"

The cat lies down on the road, closes her eyes and goes back to sleep.

Chapter 3: The Cow and the Pig

The Gingerbread Man runs down the long road. The sun is warm and the sky is blue. Birds sing in the trees.

"What a big world," he says. "There is so much to see!"

He sees yellow, pink and white flowers. He sees a little river, but he does not go near it.

"Water and gingerbread are not friends," he says.

Then he comes to a field. There is a big brown cow in the field, and she eats the green grass. She looks up.

"Moo," says the cow. "Good morning, little man. What are you?"

"I am the Gingerbread Man," he says.

The cow comes to the fence and smells him. "Mmm, you smell of ginger and honey," she says. "I am tired of grass. Grass, grass, grass, every day! Stop, little man, because I want to eat you."

The Gingerbread Man laughs. "Eat me? No, thank you! An old woman runs after me. An old man and a fat cat run after me too. They can't catch me, and you can't catch me either!"

And he sings his song. "Run, run, as fast as you can, you can't catch me, I'm the Gingerbread Man!"

He runs away and the cow runs after him. Her big feet go thump, thump, thump, and her bell goes ding, dong, ding. But the cow is big and heavy, and the Gingerbread Man is small and fast.

He runs under the fence. The cow is too big for the fence, so she stops.

"Moo, come back," she says. But the Gingerbread Man does not come back.

Next he comes to a farm. There is a fat pink pig in the mud. He rolls in the mud and he is very happy.

"Oink," says the pig. "Hello, little brown man. Do you want to play in the mud with me?"

"No, thank you," says the Gingerbread Man. "Mud and gingerbread are not friends."

"Gingerbread?" says the pig. He stands up. "You are gingerbread?"

"Yes, I am the Gingerbread Man."

The pig licks his lips. "I love gingerbread," he says. "I eat everything. I eat apples and potatoes, and I eat old shoes. And now I want to eat you!"

"Old shoes? Yuck," says the Gingerbread Man. "No, thank you! An old woman, an old man, a cat and a cow run after me. They can't catch me, and you can't catch me either!"

And he sings his song. "Run, run, as fast as you can, you can't catch me, I'm the Gingerbread Man!"

The pig runs after him, but the pig is full of mud. His feet slip and he falls on his nose. Splash! Now his nose is full of mud too.

"Oink, oink, that is not fair," says the pig.

The Gingerbread Man laughs and laughs. Then he runs on down the road.

Chapter 4: The Horse and the Hay

The road goes up a hill, and the Gingerbread Man walks up the hill. He is a little tired now, because his legs are small and the hill is big.

At the top of the hill there is a tall white horse with a long tail. He stands under a tree.

"Hello, little man," says the horse. "Where do you want to go?"

"I don't know," says the Gingerbread Man. "I want to see the world."

"The world is very big, and you are very small," says the horse. "You look tired. Come and sit on my back, and I can carry you."

The Gingerbread Man looks at the horse. The horse has big teeth, very big, very white teeth.

"Hmm," says the Gingerbread Man. "Why do you have such big teeth?"

"For apples and for carrots," says the horse. "And..." He smells the Gingerbread Man. "And for gingerbread! Stop, little man, because I want to eat you!"

"Ha! Everybody wants to eat me," says the Gingerbread Man. "An old woman, an old man, a cat, a cow and a pig run after me. They can't catch me, and you can't catch me either!"

And he sings his song. "Run, run, as fast as you can, you can't catch me, I'm the Gingerbread Man!"

He runs down the hill and the horse runs after him. The horse is very fast. Clip, clop, clip, clop! He comes closer and closer.

The Gingerbread Man sees a field of hay. Hay is dry grass for animals, and it is tall and yellow. The Gingerbread Man jumps into the hay, and now nobody can see him.

The horse stops. He looks left and he looks right. He looks in the hay. But there is a lot of hay, and the Gingerbread Man is very small.

"Where is he?" asks the horse.

Three farmers work in the field. They cut the hay and put it in a big cart. One farmer puts a big fork into the hay, and he finds the Gingerbread Man.

"Look," says the farmer. "A little cake man!"

"A cake man?" say the other farmers. They put down their tools. "It is lunch time and we are very hungry. We want cake!"

"Oh no, not again," says the Gingerbread Man.

He jumps out of the hay and runs across the field. The three farmers run after him, and the horse runs after the farmers.

Soon the farmers are tired, and they sit down in the hay.

"Our lunch runs away," says one farmer. "That is new for me!"

The horse stops too. He looks at the tall yellow hay and takes a big bite.

"Well," says the horse, "hay is good too."

Chapter 5: The Village

The Gingerbread Man comes to a village. There are little houses with red roofs and a church with a tall tower. There is a shop with a big window.

The shop is a bakery. There are breads and cakes in the window. There are small gingerbread men too, all in a line. They do not move and they do not talk.

The Gingerbread Man looks at them. "Hello, my brothers," he says.

The gingerbread men say nothing.

"They can't talk and they can't run," says the Gingerbread Man. "They only sit in a window all day. How sad!"

The baker comes out of the shop. He is a big man with flour on his nose.

"What is this?" says the baker. "A talking gingerbread man! A running gingerbread man! I can put you in my window, and everybody can come and see you. I can sell you for ten gold coins!"

"Sell me? No, thank you," says the Gingerbread Man. "I don't want to sit in a window all day."

The baker opens his big hands. "Come here, little man!"

Then the school bell rings. Ding, ding, ding! The school door opens, and lots of children run out into the street.

"Look, a gingerbread man," says a little girl. "And he can run!"

"Let's catch him," says a boy.

Now the baker and all the children run after the Gingerbread Man.

"Everybody wants me, but nobody can catch me," he says. "An old woman, an old man, a cat, a cow, a pig, a horse and three farmers run after me. They can't catch me, and you can't catch me either!"

And he sings his song. "Run, run, as fast as you can, you can't catch me, I'm the Gingerbread Man!"

He runs past the church and past the last house. The children laugh and wave, and they are very happy. The baker is not happy. He goes back to his shop with flour on his nose and nothing in his hands.

Chapter 6: The Forest

The road goes into a big, dark forest with very tall trees. The forest is quiet, and there is no sun here. The Gingerbread Man walks between the trees.

"I don't like this forest," he says. "It is cold and dark."

Then he hears a big, deep sound. Grrrr, grrrr!

Under a tree there is a very big brown bear. He sleeps and he snores. Grrrr, grrrr!

"Shh," says the Gingerbread Man to himself. "Walk very quietly."

He walks on his little feet. Step, step, step. But there are dry leaves on the ground. Crack! The bear opens his eyes.

"Who is there?" says the bear. He sits up and sees the Gingerbread Man. "Oh, hello! You are brown, like me. Are you a little bear?"

"No, I am the Gingerbread Man," he says.

The bear smells the air. "Ginger and honey," he says. "I love honey! Stop, little man, because I want to eat you."

"Everybody says that," says the Gingerbread Man. "An old woman, an old man, a cat, a cow, a pig, a horse, three farmers, a baker and lots of children run after me. They can't catch me, and you can't catch me either!"

And he sings his song. "Run, run, as fast as you can, you can't catch me, I'm the Gingerbread Man!"

He runs between the trees and the bear runs after him. But the bear is very sleepy and very big. He runs into a tree. Bang! Lots of leaves fall on his head.

"Oh, my head," says the bear. He lies down again. "Maybe I can eat him tomorrow." Then he goes back to sleep. Grrrr, grrrr!

The Gingerbread Man laughs. "Nobody can catch me," he says. "I am the fastest in the world!"

But he is very tired now and his feet hurt. And one of his white buttons is not there.

Chapter 7: The River

The Gingerbread Man walks out of the forest. The sun is low in the sky, and the sky is orange and pink. It is evening.

In front of him there is a wide, deep river with fast water. On the other side there are green hills.

"I want to go there," says the Gingerbread Man. "But how? I can't swim, and water and gingerbread are not friends."

There is no bridge and there is no boat.

Behind him he hears the animals and the people. They are far away, but they come closer and closer. There is the old woman, the old man and the cat. There is the cow and the pig, the horse and the farmers. There is the baker with the children. And there is the bear.

"Oh dear, what can I do?" says the Gingerbread Man.

Then a voice says, "Good evening, little friend."

He turns. There is a fox with red fur, a white chest and a long, beautiful tail. His eyes are clever, and he smiles a big smile.

"Good evening," says the Gingerbread Man. "You can't catch me, because I am the Gingerbread Man!"

"Catch you? Oh no," says the fox. "I don't want to catch you. I don't eat gingerbread, I only eat chickens. I am your friend."

"Really?" says the Gingerbread Man.

"Really," says the fox. "You look sad. What is the problem?"

"I want to go across the river," says the Gingerbread Man. "But I can't swim, and everybody runs after me. Listen!"

The fox listens. He hears the people and the animals, and he smiles again.

"I can swim, and I swim very well," says the fox. "Jump on my tail, and I can carry you across the river."

The Gingerbread Man looks at the fox and then at the river. He listens to the people and the animals. They are close now.

"You don't want to eat me?" he asks.

"No, no, no," says the fox. "I am a good fox and a kind fox. Come, little friend. Quick!"

The Gingerbread Man thinks for a minute. Then he jumps onto the fox's tail.

"Hold on tight," says the fox.

The fox walks into the cold river and swims. His legs move under the water. Slowly, slowly, they go across the river.

"Thank you, Mr Fox," says the Gingerbread Man. "You are very kind."

"You are welcome," says the fox, and he smiles his big smile.

Chapter 8: Snip, Snap!

The fox swims to the middle of the river, where the water is deeper. Now the fox's tail is low in the water.

"Little friend, my tail is wet," says the fox. "You are too heavy for my tail, so jump onto my back."

The Gingerbread Man jumps onto the fox's back. "Thank you," he says. "Now my feet are dry."

The fox swims on and the water is deeper and deeper. Now the fox's back is under the water too.

"Little friend, my back is wet," says the fox. "You are too heavy for my back, so jump onto my head."

The Gingerbread Man jumps onto the fox's head. "Thank you," he says. "Now my feet are dry."

The fox swims on and the water is very deep now. Only the fox's nose is out of the water.

"Little friend, my head is under the water," says the fox. "Quick, jump onto my nose!"

The Gingerbread Man jumps onto the fox's nose. "Thank you," he says. "Now my feet are dry."

Then the fox throws his head up into the air, and the Gingerbread Man flies up. Then he falls down, down, down, into the fox's open mouth.

Snip! "Oh, my legs," says the Gingerbread Man.

Snap! "Oh, oh, my arms," says the Gingerbread Man.

Snip, snap! "Oh, oh, oh, my head," says the Gingerbread Man.

And then the Gingerbread Man says nothing at all.

The fox swims to the other side of the river and walks out of the water. He licks his lips.

"Mmm, ginger and honey, very good," says the fox. He smiles. "Fast legs are good, little friend. But a clever head is better."

Then the fox runs up the green hill and goes home to his warm little hole.

On the other side of the river, the old woman and the old man arrive with the cat. The cow, the pig and the horse arrive with the farmers. The baker and the children arrive with the bear. They all look across the river, but there is nobody there. There is no Gingerbread Man.

"Where is he?" asks a little girl.

The old woman looks at the fox on the hill, and she understands. "Oh dear," she says. "My little Gingerbread Man."

The children are sad, and the old woman is sad too. Then she looks at the children. There are so many of them, and they are so hungry.

"Come with me, all of you," she says. "Come to my house."

They all walk back to the little house at the end of the long road. The old woman goes into the kitchen and takes her big bowl. She puts in flour, sugar, butter, an egg, honey and ginger, and she mixes and mixes.

This time she makes lots of little gingerbread men. And this time she closes the kitchen door and the windows too.

The gingerbread men come out of the oven. They do not talk and they do not run. They are only very, very good.

The children eat, and the old man eats three.

Now the little house is not quiet. Every day after school the children come and sit in the kitchen. They laugh and sing, and the old woman makes gingerbread for everybody.

The old man looks at her. "We have no children," he says. "And now we have lots of children!"

The old woman smiles. "Yes," she says. "And they don't run away."

The End
