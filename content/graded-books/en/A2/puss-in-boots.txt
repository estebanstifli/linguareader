Chapter 1: A Cat for a Fortune

Once upon a time, there was an old miller. He lived in a small village in France, next to a river. Every day, the big wheel of his mill turned in the water, and every day the miller made flour from wheat. He was not a rich man, but he worked hard all his life.

The miller had three sons. The oldest son was strong and serious. The second son was clever with money. The youngest son was called Pierre. He was kind and gentle, and he loved to sit by the river and dream.

One cold winter, the old miller became very ill. He called his three sons to his bed.

"My boys," he said in a weak voice, "I have very little to give you. Listen carefully. The mill is for my oldest son. The donkey is for my second son. And the cat is for Pierre."

A few days later, the old man died. The three brothers were very sad. After the funeral, the oldest brother took the mill. The second brother took the donkey. And Pierre got the cat.

That evening, the two older brothers sat by the fire in the mill. They counted their father's old sacks and talked about money. They did not ask Pierre to sit with them.

"The mill is mine now," said the oldest brother. "I am sorry, Pierre, but there is no room for you here. You must find another place to live."

"And I need the donkey for my work," said the second brother. "I cannot give him to you."

Pierre did not argue with them. He put his few things in an old bag: a shirt, a knife, a piece of bread and his father's hat. Then he called the cat and walked out into the cold night. He found an empty little house at the edge of the forest. The roof had holes and the door did not close well, but it was better than nothing.

The cat was a big grey animal with green eyes and a long tail. He usually slept by the fire or caught mice in the kitchen.

Pierre sat on a stone outside the mill and looked at the cat. He felt very unhappy.

"My brothers are lucky," he said. "My oldest brother can make flour with the mill. My other brother can carry things to the market with the donkey. Together, they can make good money. But what can I do with a cat? I can eat him, and I can make a pair of warm gloves from his fur. After that, I have nothing. I am going to die of hunger."

The cat was lying near him in the sun. His eyes were closed, but he heard every word. Slowly, he opened one green eye. Then he sat up and looked at his young master.

"Please do not worry, master," said the cat in a calm, clear voice.

Pierre almost fell off his stone. "You can talk!" he cried.

"Of course I can talk," said the cat. "I am not an ordinary cat. Please do not eat me, and please do not make gloves from my fur. I have a better idea. Give me a bag and a pair of boots. Good leather boots, for walking in the forest. Then you will see. I am worth much more than a mill and a donkey."

Pierre did not really believe the cat. But he remembered something. This cat was very clever. He often caught rats and mice in very smart ways. Sometimes he hung upside down from a shelf. Sometimes he hid in the flour and waited without moving.

"Well," said Pierre, "I have nothing to lose."

He took his last few coins and went to the village. He bought a strong cloth bag. Then he went to the shoemaker and bought a small pair of red leather boots with a little feather on each one.

The cat put on the boots. He walked up and down in front of Pierre. He looked very proud and very elegant. He moved his long tail from side to side, and he turned around to look at his boots from every side.

"Excellent," said the cat. "From today, you can call me Puss in Boots."

Chapter 2: Presents for the King

The next morning, Puss in Boots got up early. He put some lettuce and some carrots in his bag. Then he put the bag over his shoulder and walked into the forest.

In the forest, there were many rabbits. Puss found a good place near their holes. He opened the bag on the ground and put the lettuce and carrots inside. Then he lay down next to it and closed his eyes. He did not move at all. He looked like a dead cat.

After some time, a young rabbit came out of its hole. It was not very clever. It smelled the fresh carrots and hopped closer and closer. Then it jumped into the bag to eat them.

Quickly, Puss pulled the strings of the bag. The rabbit was caught!

Puss was very happy. But he did not take the rabbit home to Pierre. Instead, he walked out of the forest and along the road to the king's palace.

The palace was very large and beautiful. It had white towers, golden windows and a big garden full of roses. Two guards stood at the gate with long spears.

"Stop!" said one of the guards. "Who are you, and what do you want?"

"I am Puss in Boots," said the cat. "I want to speak to the king."

The guards looked at each other. A cat in boots! They laughed, but they were also curious. So they took him inside.

Puss walked into a big room with a high ceiling. The king sat on a golden throne. He was a round, friendly man with a white beard. Next to him sat his daughter, the princess. She had long dark hair and bright, intelligent eyes.

Puss in Boots took off his hat and bowed very low.

"Your Majesty," he said, "I bring you a present from my master, the Marquis of Carabas. He hunted this rabbit in his own forest this morning. He hopes you enjoy it."

Of course, the Marquis of Carabas did not exist. Puss invented the name on the way to the palace. He thought it sounded very important.

The king did not know any Marquis of Carabas. But he did not want to say so. He loved rabbit stew.

"How kind!" said the king. "Please tell your master thank you. He is a very generous man."

The princess looked at the cat with interest. "Who is this Marquis?" she asked. "I want to know more about him."

"One day, Your Highness," said Puss, and he bowed again.

A few days later, Puss went to a wheat field. He hid in the tall wheat with his open bag. This time, he put some grain inside. Soon two fat birds walked into the bag. Puss pulled the strings and caught them both. Again, he took them to the king.

"Another present from the Marquis of Carabas," he said.

The king was very pleased. He gave Puss a glass of milk and a piece of cake. This went on for two or three months. Every week, Puss in Boots brought a present to the palace. And every week, the king heard about the wonderful Marquis of Carabas.

Meanwhile, Pierre stayed at home. He did not know anything about the presents. He only knew that his cat came home every evening with a big smile on his face.

Life in the little house was not easy. Pierre had very little food. Every morning, he went into the forest to look for wood and mushrooms. Every evening, he sat by a small fire and felt hungry.

One night, he looked at the cat with sad eyes. "Puss," he said, "where do you go every day? I bought you boots with my last coins. But we still have nothing to eat."

"Patience, master," said Puss. "A good hunter is never in a hurry. Wait a little longer. Soon you will have more food than you can eat."

The next evening, Puss came home with a fat fish and a loaf of bread from the palace kitchen. The cooks liked the polite cat in boots, and they often gave him things. Pierre and his cat ate a wonderful dinner together. For the first time in many weeks, Pierre went to bed with a full stomach.

"Maybe this cat is right," he thought before he fell asleep. "Maybe he really is worth more than a mill and a donkey."

Chapter 3: The River

One day, Puss heard some important news in the palace kitchen. The cooks were talking about it.

"Tomorrow the king and the princess are going to take a drive in the royal carriage," said the head cook. "They want to go along the river and look at the countryside."

Puss in Boots ran home as fast as he could. His boots made a loud noise on the road.

"Master!" he shouted. "Listen to me! Tomorrow you must do exactly what I say. If you do it, your fortune is made."

Pierre was surprised, but he trusted his cat now. "All right," he said. "What do I have to do?"

"Tomorrow morning, go to the river. I will show you a place. Take off your clothes and swim in the water. Then wait. That is all."

"Swim?" said Pierre. "In the river? Why?"

"Do not ask questions," said Puss. "Just do it."

The next morning, Pierre and the cat went down to the river. Puss chose a place near the road. Pierre took off his old clothes and jumped into the water. The water was very cold. Pierre shook and his teeth made a noise.

While Pierre was swimming, Puss took his old clothes. They were dirty and full of holes. He hid them under a big stone.

Soon they heard horses and wheels on the road. The royal carriage was coming. It was blue and gold, and six white horses pulled it.

Puss ran into the middle of the road. He waved his arms and shouted as loudly as possible.

"Help! Help! The Marquis of Carabas is drowning!"

The king looked out of the window of the carriage. He recognized the cat in boots. This was the cat with all the wonderful presents!

"Stop the carriage!" ordered the king. "Guards, quickly! Help the Marquis of Carabas!"

The guards jumped down and ran to the river. They pulled Pierre out of the water. He was wet, cold and very confused.

Puss in Boots came to the carriage and bowed to the king.

"Thank you, Your Majesty," he said. "A terrible thing happened. My master was swimming in the river. Then some thieves came and stole all his clothes. Poor man! He cannot go anywhere now."

"How terrible!" said the king. He immediately sent a servant back to the palace. "Bring my best clothes for the Marquis," he said. "Hurry!"

The servant came back with a beautiful suit. It was made of blue silk, with gold buttons and a white shirt. There was also a hat with a feather and a pair of shiny black shoes.

Pierre put on the clothes. Pierre was young and good-looking, and in these fine clothes he looked like a real prince.

The princess looked at him through the window. Then she looked at him again. She smiled, and Pierre smiled back. She thought he was the most handsome man in the world.

"Please, Marquis," said the king, "come into the carriage with us. We are driving along the river. You can keep us company."

Pierre did not know what to say. He looked at Puss. The cat nodded. So Pierre climbed into the royal carriage and sat next to the princess.

Inside, the carriage was soft and warm. The seats were made of red velvet, and there was a little basket of fruit on a small table. It was the first time in his life in a royal carriage. Pierre did not know what to do with his hands. He sat very straight and tried to look like a rich man. He did not want to make a mistake.

"Are you cold, Marquis?" asked the princess kindly. "You were in the river for a long time."

"A little," said Pierre. "But I feel much better now. Thank you, Your Highness."

The princess gave him an apple from the basket. Pierre was very hungry, and he ate it in four bites. The princess laughed, and soon Pierre laughed too. After that, he felt more relaxed.

Chapter 4: The Farmers in the Fields

As the carriage drove away, Puss in Boots ran ahead along the road. He ran faster than the horses. His red boots flew over the stones.

Soon he came to a big field. Some farmers were cutting the grass.

Puss stopped in front of them. He stood very tall and tried to look dangerous.

"Good people," he said, "listen to me carefully. In a few minutes, the king is going to pass here in his carriage. He is going to ask you a question. 'Whose field is this?' You must answer, 'It belongs to the Marquis of Carabas.' If you do not say this, you are all going to be chopped into small pieces like meat for a pie!"

The farmers were frightened. They knew a lot about cows, horses and sheep, but this was their first cat in boots. And this cat talked about chopping people into pieces! So they agreed.

A few minutes later, the royal carriage arrived. The king looked out of the window.

"Good morning!" he called to the farmers. "What a beautiful field! Whose field is this?"

"It belongs to the Marquis of Carabas, Your Majesty," answered the farmers all together.

The king turned to Pierre. "You have a very fine field, Marquis," he said.

Pierre was very surprised, but he smiled politely. "Yes, Your Majesty," he said. "It gives good grass every year."

Meanwhile, Puss in Boots was already far ahead. He came to a large field of golden wheat. Many people were working there. He said the same thing to them.

"When the king comes, tell him the wheat belongs to the Marquis of Carabas. If you do not, you will be chopped into little pieces!"

When the carriage passed, the king asked again. "Whose wheat is this?"

"It belongs to the Marquis of Carabas, Your Majesty!" the workers answered.

The king was more and more impressed. Everywhere they went, he asked the same question. And everywhere they went, he heard the same answer. The meadows, the forests, the orchards with apple trees and the vineyards all belonged to the Marquis of Carabas.

"Marquis," said the king, "you are a very rich man. I did not know this."

The princess was not thinking about fields and forests. She was talking to Pierre. He told her about the river, the birds and the stars. He was not like the other rich men she knew. He did not talk about money all the time. He listened to her. She liked him very much.

But there was a problem. The fields did not really belong to the Marquis. They belonged to someone very different.

Chapter 5: The Ogre's Castle

All the land around the river belonged to an ogre. An ogre is a huge, ugly monster, and this ogre was the richest and most terrible of all. He lived in a big castle on top of a hill. The farmers worked for him, and they were all afraid of him. Some people said he ate children for breakfast. Nobody went near his castle. Even the birds did not sing on his hill.

The ogre also had a special magic power. He could change into any animal he wanted.

Puss in Boots knew all of this. He ran up the hill to the castle. It was dark and grey, with tall black towers. Puss knocked on the enormous door.

The door opened slowly. The ogre was standing there. He was three times taller than a man. He had a huge head, small red eyes and very big teeth.

"Who are you?" the ogre asked in a deep voice. "Why are you knocking on my door?"

Puss in Boots bowed very politely. "Good day, sir," he said. "I was passing near your castle. I could not go past without visiting you. People everywhere talk about your great powers. I wanted to see them with my own eyes."

The ogre liked compliments very much. Not many people visited him. He smiled, and his big teeth shone.

"Come in, come in," he said. "Sit down."

Puss went into a large hall and sat on a chair. The table was full of food. The ogre was preparing a big dinner.

"Sir," said Puss, "people say you can change into any animal. They say you can even become a lion or an elephant. But I do not believe it. It is impossible."

"Impossible?" shouted the ogre. "Watch this!"

Suddenly, the ogre disappeared, and a huge lion stood in his place. It had a great golden mane and long sharp teeth. It roared so loudly that the windows shook.

Puss was so frightened that he jumped out of the window and climbed onto the roof. His boots were not good for climbing, and he almost fell. He held on to the roof with all his strength.

After a moment, the lion changed back into the ogre. The ogre laughed and laughed. Puss came down from the roof very carefully. His heart was beating fast.

"That was amazing," said Puss in a shaky voice. "You really frightened me."

"Of course," said the ogre proudly.

"But," said Puss, "it is easy for a big creature to become another big creature. Some people say you can also change into a very small animal. A rat, or even a little mouse. But I am sure that is impossible. Nobody can do that."

"Impossible again?" roared the ogre. "Nothing is impossible for me!"

In a second, the ogre disappeared. On the floor, there was a tiny grey mouse. It ran across the floor.

Puss in Boots did not wait. He jumped on the mouse and ate it in one bite.

That was the end of the terrible ogre.

Chapter 6: Welcome to the Castle

Puss in Boots cleaned his whiskers. Then he went to find the ogre's servants. They were all in the kitchen, and they were all shaking with fear.

"Good news," said Puss. "The ogre is gone. He will never hurt you again. You have a new master now. His name is the Marquis of Carabas, and he is a good and kind man."

The servants could not believe it. They laughed, they cried and they danced around the kitchen.

An old woman with white hair came forward. She was the ogre's cook. For twenty years, she prepared his terrible dinners.

"Thank you, Master Cat," she said with tears in her eyes. "Every day we were afraid. When the ogre was angry, he shouted and broke the furniture. He never paid us. He never said thank you. Now we can live in peace."

A young boy came forward too. He worked in the stables with the horses. "Is the new master really kind?" he asked. "Does he shout?"

"He never shouts," said Puss. "He likes to sit by the river and listen to the birds. You will like him very much."

"Now, quickly," said Puss. "The Marquis is coming with the king and the princess. Prepare the castle. Open all the windows. Clean the tables. Put flowers everywhere. And prepare a wonderful dinner!"

The servants worked very fast. They opened the dark curtains, and the sunlight came into the castle. They put red and white roses in every room. They prepared the ogre's dinner, but they changed it a little. There was roast chicken, fresh bread, soup, cheese, fruit and three kinds of cake.

A short time later, Puss heard the wheels of the royal carriage on the bridge. He ran out of the castle and stood at the big front door. He bowed very low as the carriage stopped.

"Your Majesty," he said, "welcome to the castle of my master, the Marquis of Carabas."

The king got out of the carriage. He looked up at the tall towers and the large windows.

"What!" he cried. "This castle belongs to you too, Marquis? It is magnificent! Nothing is more beautiful than this castle, not even my own palace."

Pierre was speechless. He looked at Puss in Boots. The cat only winked at him. Pierre did not understand what was happening. But he decided to trust his cat one more time.

Pierre gave his hand to the princess and helped her out of the carriage. Then they all followed the king into the great hall.

The dinner was ready on the long table. There were candles and flowers and silver plates. The king sat down and ate and drank. He was in a very good mood. He laughed at all the jokes, and he had three pieces of cake.

The princess did not eat very much. She was too busy talking to Pierre. She asked him about his life. Pierre did not tell her everything. But he told her about his father, the mill and the river. He told her that he loved simple things, like a warm fire and a good book.

"I like simple things too," said the princess quietly.

Chapter 7: The Wedding

After dinner, the king sat back in his chair. He looked at the Marquis. Then he looked at his daughter. He saw how she smiled at the young man. He saw how the young man smiled back.

"Marquis," said the king, "you are rich, you are handsome and you are very polite. And I can see that my daughter likes you very much. Do you want to marry her?"

Pierre was quiet for a moment. He looked at the princess.

"Your Majesty," he said, "I want to marry your daughter. But only if she wants to marry me."

The princess laughed. "Of course I do!" she said.

The king was delighted. "Wonderful!" he cried. "The wedding will be this Sunday!"

Later that evening, Pierre found Puss in the garden. The cat was sitting on the wall and looking at the moon.

"Puss," said Pierre, "I do not understand anything. The fields, the castle, the servants... How did this happen? I am not a marquis. I am only the son of a poor miller."

"You are a good man, master," said Puss. "You are kind and honest, and the princess loves you for that. The rest is just a little help from your cat."

Pierre thought about this. "I must tell the princess the truth one day," he said.

"Yes," said the cat. "And one day you will. But first, enjoy your wedding."

The wedding was on Sunday, as the king said. It was the most beautiful wedding in the history of the kingdom. The bells of every church rang all morning. People came from all the villages to see the young couple. The farmers came too. Now they were free from the ogre, and they were very happy.

The princess wore a long white dress. Pierre wore a blue suit with gold buttons. Pierre's two brothers were there too. They could not believe their eyes.

"Our little brother is marrying a princess!" said the oldest brother. "And all because of a cat!"

Puss in Boots sat in the first row. He wore his red boots, cleaned and polished, and a new hat with a long white feather. Many people looked at him and smiled.

After the wedding, there was a big party in the castle. There was music and dancing until late at night. The king danced with everybody. Even Puss in Boots danced with the cook.

Chapter 8: A Happy Ending

Pierre and the princess lived in the castle on the hill. Pierre was a good master. He was fair with the farmers and paid them well for their work. The land became green and rich again. Nobody was afraid anymore.

One evening, Pierre told the princess the whole story. He told her about the mill, the donkey and the cat. He told her about the river and the clothes under the stone. He was afraid that she was going to be angry.

But the princess only laughed.

"I fell in love with you, not with your castle," she said. "And I think your cat is the cleverest cat in the world."

Pierre's brothers came to visit very often. The oldest brother still worked at the mill. The second brother started a small business with his donkey. Pierre helped them both, and the three brothers were good friends again.

When the old king died, Pierre and the princess became the new king and queen. They were wise and fair, and the people loved them.

And what about Puss in Boots? He became a great lord. He had a room in the castle with a soft bed and a big window. He ate fresh fish every day from a silver plate. He wore a new pair of boots for every season.

He did not need to catch mice anymore. He had plenty of food. But sometimes, late at night, he went down to the kitchen. He took off his boots, waited very quietly in a dark corner and chased the mice just for fun.

After all, he was still a cat.

The End
