Chapter 1: The Merchant and His Daughters

Once upon a time, in a big city by the sea, there lived a rich merchant. He had six children, three sons and three daughters.

The merchant loved his children very much. He gave them good teachers, fine clothes and beautiful books, because he wanted them to be clever and happy.

His daughters were all very pretty, but the youngest daughter was the prettiest of all. When she was a little girl, everyone called her "Little Beauty". Later, they only called her Beauty, and the name stayed with her.

Her two older sisters did not like this, because they were jealous of her. "Why is she Beauty?" said the oldest sister. "We are pretty too!"

"Yes," said the second sister. "And we wear nicer dresses than she does."

The two older sisters were proud, and they did not want to talk to the daughters of other merchants. They only wanted to talk to rich and important people, and every evening they went to parties, to balls and to the theatre. They laughed at poor people in the street.

Beauty was different, and she was kind to everyone. She liked parties, but she liked books more, and she often sat in a quiet room and read for hours.

"Look at her," said the oldest sister. "She is reading again, and it is so boring!"

Many young men came to the merchant's house. They wanted to marry his daughters, because the family was rich.

"I am not going to marry a merchant," said the oldest sister. "I want to marry a duke."

"And I want a count," said the second sister, "or a prince, and nothing less."

Some young men asked Beauty to marry them too, and she always thanked them politely. "You are very kind," she said. "But I am too young, and I want to stay with my father for a few more years."

Then one day, everything changed.

A man ran into the merchant's office, and his face was white. "Sir," he said, "I have terrible news. There was a great storm at sea, and your ships are gone, all of them."

The merchant sat down slowly. "All of them?" he said.

"Yes, sir, and I am very sorry."

The merchant's ships carried all his money, so without the ships he had nothing. In one day, the rich merchant became a poor man.

He sold the big house in the city, the horses and the carriages. He sold the fine furniture and the paintings, and he only kept a small house in the country, far from the city.

That evening he spoke to his children. "My dear children," he said sadly. "We are poor now, and we must go and live in the country. We must work on the land like farmers, because it is the only way."

The two older sisters began to cry. "No!" said the oldest. "We are not going to the country! Our friends are going to help us, because they love us."

"Yes," said the second. "Many young men want to marry us, and we can marry one of them and stay in the city."

But they were wrong, and their friends did not help them. When they heard the news, they said, "Good! Those girls were always so proud, and now they can learn to milk cows."

And the young men did not come back, because they did not want to marry poor girls.

But many people were sorry for Beauty. "Poor Beauty," they said. "She was always so kind to everyone."

Some young men still wanted to marry her, even without any money. But Beauty said no again. "My father is sad and tired now," she said. "He needs me, so I am going with him."

So the merchant and his six children left the city. They travelled for two days in an old cart, and at last they arrived at a small house near a forest.

Chapter 2: A Rose for Beauty

Life in the country was hard, and the merchant and his three sons worked in the fields all day. They planted wheat in the fields and cut wood for the fire.

Beauty got up every morning at four o'clock, and she cleaned the house and made breakfast for the family. Then she washed the clothes, fed the chickens and cooked the dinner.

At first it was very difficult for her, and her hands hurt and her back was tired. In the evenings she sometimes cried a little in her room. But she said to herself, "Crying is not going to help me. Our money is gone, so I must be happy without it."

After two months she was stronger, her cheeks were pink and she sang while she worked. When the work was finished, she read her books or played her old harpsichord. Sometimes she sang songs to her father in the evening.

Her two sisters did not work at all, and they got up at ten o'clock in the morning. They walked around the garden in their old city dresses. All day they talked about their lost clothes and their lost friends.

"Look at our little sister," said the oldest sister. "She is happy to be a servant, so she must be stupid."

"Yes," said the second. "She works like a farm girl, because she was born for this life."

The merchant knew the truth, and he loved Beauty more every day. "My daughter is not stupid," he thought. "She is good and brave, and her sisters are lazy."

They lived like this for a year, and then one morning a letter arrived. The merchant read it and jumped up from his chair.

"Children!" he cried. "Wonderful news! One of my ships did not sink! It arrived in the port last week, and it is full of goods!"

The two older sisters screamed with happiness. "We are going to be rich again!" said the oldest. "We can go back to the city!"

"I must go to the port at once and see my ship," said the merchant. "Then I can sell the goods." He looked at his daughters. "What can I bring you from the city, my dears?"

"Oh, Father!" said the oldest sister. "Bring me a blue silk dress and a hat with feathers."

"And bring me a red velvet dress," said the second sister, "and gloves, and a necklace, and new shoes."

The merchant turned to his youngest daughter. "And you, Beauty? What do you want?"

Beauty thought for a moment. She did not want to ask for much, because her father needed the money for the family. But she did not want her sisters to look bad, either.

"Father, you are very kind," she said. "Please bring me a rose. There are no roses here in the country, and I miss them."

Her sisters laughed. "A rose!" said the oldest. "She only wants to look better than us, little Miss Perfect!"

The merchant smiled at Beauty. "A rose it is," he said.

The next morning he rode away on his old horse. The journey to the port was long, and when he arrived there was more bad news. Many people wanted his money, because he owed money to other merchants from the old days. They took everything from the ship, and in the end the merchant was as poor as before.

"I am going home with nothing," he said sadly. "Not one silk dress, not one velvet dress, nothing."

He got on his horse and started the long journey home.

Chapter 3: The Palace in the Snow

The merchant was only thirty miles from his home. But it was winter, and the weather was terrible. The snow began to fall and the wind blew hard, and soon the merchant could not see the road.

He rode into a big dark forest. The trees were tall and black. The snow fell and fell, and the wind blew and blew.

"I am lost," said the merchant, "and I can't see anything."

Then he heard wolves, and they were howling somewhere in the forest. "Owooo! Owooo!" The merchant was afraid, and his horse was afraid too.

"I am going to die here," he thought. "I am going to die from the cold, or the wolves are going to eat me."

He rode on and on, and suddenly he saw a light between the trees. It was far away, but it was bright, so he rode towards it.

The light came from a great palace with lights in all the windows. The gates were open, but there was nobody there.

The merchant rode into the courtyard. There was a stable with an open door, and inside it was warm. There was fresh hay and oats for the horse. The hungry horse began to eat at once.

The merchant tied the horse and walked to the palace. The big door was open, so he went inside. He walked through a long hall, and then he came into a large room with a big fire. There was a table by the fire, and on it there was a fine dinner for one person.

"Hello?" called the merchant. "Is anybody here?"

Nobody answered.

The merchant was wet and cold, so he stood by the fire and dried his clothes. "The owner of this house is going to come soon," he thought. "I can explain everything to him. I am sure he is a kind man."

He waited and waited. The clock struck nine, then ten, then eleven, but nobody came.

At last the merchant was too hungry. He sat down at the table and ate a chicken. He was still shaking with fear, but the food was delicious. Then he drank a glass of wine, and he felt braver.

He walked through the palace. There were many beautiful rooms with fine furniture. In one room there was a big soft bed. The merchant was very tired, so he closed the door, lay down and fell asleep at once.

When he woke up, it was ten o'clock in the morning. He looked around, and his old clothes were gone! There were new clothes on a chair, and they were clean and fine.

"This palace belongs to a good fairy," he thought. "She feels sorry for me."

He looked out of the window, and the snow was gone. Outside there was a beautiful garden full of flowers. It was summer in the garden!

In the big room there was a hot breakfast on the table, with hot chocolate. "Thank you, good fairy," said the merchant. He ate his breakfast and went outside to find his horse.

On the way to the stable, he walked under some roses. They were red and pink and white, and they smelled wonderful.

"Beauty!" he thought. "Beauty wants a rose." He reached up and picked one.

At that moment he heard a terrible noise, a great roar, and the merchant turned around.

A horrible creature was walking towards him. It was very big, and it had fur and claws and a terrible face. It was a Beast.

The merchant nearly fell to the ground with fear.

"You ungrateful man!" roared the Beast. "I saved your life! I gave you food and a warm bed! And now you steal my roses! I love my roses more than anything in the world!"

The merchant fell on his knees.

"Now you must die," said the Beast, "and you have fifteen minutes."

Chapter 4: Beauty's Promise

"Please, my lord," said the merchant. "Forgive me, because I did not want to steal from you. My daughter asked me for a rose. I only wanted to make her happy."

"Do not call me my lord," said the Beast. "My name is Beast, and I don't like nice words. I like honest people. Tell me about your daughters."

So the merchant told the Beast about his family. He told him about the ships and the storm, and about Beauty and her rose.

The Beast listened, and then he said, "I am going to forgive you. But there is one condition: one of your daughters must come here. She must come freely, and she must take your place. If no daughter wants to come, you must come back yourself in three months."

The merchant did not want to give a daughter to the Beast. But he thought, "At least I can see my children one more time."

"I promise," he said. "I am going to come back in three months."

"Good," said the Beast. "But you must not go home with nothing. Go back to your room. There is an empty chest there. Fill it with anything you like. I am going to send it to your house."

Then the Beast walked away.

The merchant went back to his room. There was a big empty chest by the bed, and there was also a lot of gold on the table. He filled the chest with gold, and then he found his horse in the stable and rode away from the palace. He was very sad all the way home.

A few hours later he arrived home. His children ran out to meet him, and he gave the rose to Beauty.

"Take this rose, Beauty," he said. "Your poor father paid a high price for it."

Then he told them everything. The two older sisters began to scream and cry, but Beauty did not cry.

"Look at her!" said the oldest sister to Beauty. "This is your fault, you and your stupid rose! And you are not even crying!"

"Why must I cry?" said Beauty quietly. "My father is not going to die. The Beast wants a daughter, so I am going to go to the palace."

"No, sister!" said the three brothers. "We are going to go to the palace. We are going to fight the Beast!"

"You can't fight him," said the merchant. "He is too strong, and I am old. I am going to go back myself."

"No, Father," said Beauty. "You are not going alone, because I am coming with you. You can't stop me."

Her sisters were secretly happy. "Good," whispered the oldest. "The Beast is going to eat her. Then we are going to be the pretty ones."

That night the merchant went to his room. He found the chest of gold next to his bed! He told nobody except Beauty, and Beauty told him a secret too. "Some young men came to the house when you were away," she said. "They want to marry my sisters. Please give them some gold for their weddings. And forgive them, Father, because they are my sisters."

Three months later the day came. The two sisters rubbed their eyes with an onion, because they wanted to look sad. Their eyes were red and full of tears. But the brothers cried real tears, and so did the merchant. Only Beauty did not cry, because she did not want to make them sadder.

Beauty and her father rode to the palace, and the lights were on in every window again. In the big room there was a table with a fine dinner for two people.

"The Beast wants to make me fat before he eats me," thought Beauty. She almost laughed at her own idea.

Then they heard a loud noise, and the Beast came into the room. Beauty was very frightened when she saw his face. But she was brave and said nothing.

"Did you come here freely?" asked the Beast.

"Yes," said Beauty, and her voice was shaking.

"You are a good girl," said the Beast. "Old man, you must go home tomorrow morning. Never come back here. Goodnight, Beauty."

"Goodnight, Beast," said Beauty.

Chapter 5: Beauty's Rooms

That night Beauty had a dream, and a beautiful lady came to her. "Beauty," said the lady, "you have a good heart, because you gave your life for your father's life. You are going to get a reward one day."

In the morning Beauty told her father about the dream, and he felt a little better. But when he left the palace, they both cried a lot.

Beauty sat down in the big room and cried for a long time. Then she said to herself, "I don't have much time, because the Beast is going to eat me tonight. I am going to look around the palace, because it is very beautiful."

She walked through the rooms, and then she came to a door with gold letters on it. The letters said: "Beauty's Rooms".

She opened the door, and inside there were three lovely rooms. There was a library full of books. There was a harpsichord and a lot of music. There was a bedroom with a soft bed and a big wardrobe full of dresses.

"The Beast is not going to eat me," thought Beauty. "Nobody gives all this to a girl for only one day!"

She opened a book, and on the first page, in gold letters, she read:

"Welcome, Beauty, and do not be afraid. You are the queen here, so ask for anything you want."

"I only want to see my poor father," said Beauty sadly.

At that moment she saw a big mirror on the wall. In the mirror she saw her father's house. Her father was arriving home, and he looked very sad. Her sisters came out of the house. They tried to look sad, but they were happy inside, and Beauty could see it.

Then the picture went away.

"The Beast is kind," thought Beauty. "He wants me to be happy."

At midday there was lunch on the table, and music played, but she saw no musicians. In the afternoon she read and played the harpsichord, but nobody came.

At nine o'clock in the evening, Beauty sat down for supper. Then she heard the Beast's footsteps, and she began to shake.

"Beauty," said the Beast, "can I watch you while you eat?"

"You are the master here," said Beauty.

"No," said the Beast. "You are the mistress here. If you want me to go, I can go at once. But tell me something. Do you think I am very ugly?"

Beauty did not want to lie. "Yes, I do," she said. "I can't lie to you, but I think you are very kind."

"You are right," said the Beast. "I am ugly, and I am not clever. I know I am only a beast."

"A stupid person never says that he is stupid," said Beauty. "Stupid people think they are clever."

"Eat, Beauty," said the Beast, "because everything here is yours. I want you to be happy."

"You are very good," said Beauty. "I like your kind heart, and when I think about it, you do not look so ugly to me."

"Oh, yes," said the Beast. "My heart is good, but I am still a monster."

"Many men are worse monsters than you," said Beauty. "I prefer you to a man with a handsome face and a bad heart."

They talked for a while, and Beauty was not afraid of him now. She ate her supper and almost forgot about his face.

Then the Beast asked a question. "Beauty, will you marry me?"

Beauty was afraid again, because she did not want to make him angry. But she did not want to lie either.

"No, Beast," she said.

The Beast gave a terrible sigh. It was so loud and so sad that the whole palace shook.

"Goodnight, then, Beauty," he said quietly, and he walked out of the room. At the door, he turned and looked at her one more time.

"Poor Beast," thought Beauty. "He is so good, so why is he so ugly?"

Chapter 6: Every Night at Nine

Beauty lived in the palace for three months, and every day was quiet and pleasant. She read books, she walked in the garden and she played music. There were new dresses in her wardrobe every week.

Every evening at nine o'clock, the Beast came to her supper. They talked about many things, and he was not clever, but he was honest and good. He told her about the stars, the birds and the flowers in his garden. She told him about the city, her books and her family.

Slowly Beauty got used to his ugly face, and soon she did not see it at all. In fact, she began to look at the clock at nine o'clock. She waited for him, and she was a little sad when he was late.

"Here is my friend," she thought when she heard his footsteps.

But every night, before he left, the Beast asked the same question. "Beauty, will you marry me?"

And every night, Beauty gave the same answer. "No, Beast."

One evening she said, "Beast, you make me very sad. I want to say yes, but I can't. I am always going to be your friend, but I can't marry you. Please try to be happy with that."

"I must be happy with it," said the Beast. "I know I am horrible, but I love you very much. Promise me one thing. Promise me you are never going to leave me."

Beauty's face went red. That very day she saw her father in the mirror. He was in bed, and he was ill with sadness, because he missed her. Her sisters were married now, and her brothers were in the army. Her father was all alone.

"Beast," she said, "I can promise never to leave you forever. But I want to see my father very much, because he is ill and alone. If I can't see him, I am going to die of sadness."

"I don't want you to be sad," said the Beast. "You can go to your father, and you can stay there. Your poor Beast is going to die of sadness instead."

"No!" said Beauty. She was crying now. "I love you too much as a friend, and I don't want you to die. I promise to come back in one week. My sisters are married and my brothers are in the army. My father is alone, so let me stay with him for one week."

"You can be there tomorrow morning," said the Beast. "But remember your promise. When you want to come back, put this ring on the table before you go to sleep. Then you are going to wake up here, in the palace."

He gave her a gold ring, and then he gave his great sad sigh. "Goodbye, Beauty," he said.

That night Beauty went to bed very sad. "Poor Beast," she thought. "He is so unhappy, and it is all my fault."

Chapter 7: A Week at Home

In the morning Beauty woke up in her father's house. She rang a little bell next to her bed, and the servant came in and screamed with surprise. Her father ran into the room, and when he saw Beauty he nearly fainted with happiness. They held each other for a long, long time.

"My daughter! My dear daughter!" he cried. "You are alive!"

Then Beauty remembered something. "I have no clothes here," she said, "only my nightdress."

"Look," said the servant. "There is a big chest in the next room."

They opened the chest, and it was full of gold dresses with diamonds. "Thank you, kind Beast," said Beauty.

Her father was not ill now, because he was happy again. He sent a message to the two older sisters, and they came quickly with their husbands.

The sisters were not happy in their marriages. The oldest sister married a very handsome gentleman, but he only loved one person: himself. He looked in the mirror all day, and he did not look at his wife.

The second sister married a very clever man. But he used his clever tongue to make fun of everyone. And most of all, he made fun of his wife.

When the sisters saw Beauty, they nearly died of jealousy. She was dressed like a princess, and she was more beautiful than ever. She was happy, and she was kind to them.

They went out into the garden and cried with anger.

"Why is she so lucky?" said the oldest sister. "Are we not prettier than her?"

"Listen, sister," said the second. "I have an idea. She must go back to the Beast in one week, yes? Let us keep her here longer. Then the Beast is going to be angry, and he is going to eat her!"

"Yes!" said the oldest. "What a good idea! We must be very nice to her."

So the two sisters were very nice to Beauty. They kissed her and gave her presents. When the week was over, they started to cry and pulled their hair. "Don't go, dear sister!" they cried. "We love you so much! We can't live without you!"

Beauty was surprised and happy. "My sisters love me!" she thought, and she promised to stay one more week.

But Beauty was not happy inside. She thought about her poor Beast every day. She missed their talks at nine o'clock, and she missed his kind voice. "I love him," she said to herself. "But I don't love him as a husband. Or do I?"

On the tenth night, Beauty had a terrible dream. She was in the palace garden, and the Beast was lying on the grass. He was dying. "Beauty," he said in a weak voice. "You forgot your promise."

Beauty woke up and cried. "What am I doing here?" she said. "The Beast is so good to me. Tell me, does a girl need a handsome face or a clever tongue in a husband? No, she needs a good heart. Look at my sisters. Their husbands are handsome and clever, but my sisters are not happy. The Beast is not handsome and he is not clever, but he is good and kind. I am going to be happy with him."

She got out of bed and put the gold ring on the table. Then she went back to sleep.

Chapter 8: The Rose Garden

When Beauty woke up she was in the palace, and she was very happy to be there. She put on her prettiest dress and waited for nine o'clock.

The day was very long. Beauty walked in the garden, and she read a book, but she could not read a single page. She could only think about the Beast.

At last the clock struck nine. Beauty sat at the table and waited for his footsteps.

But the Beast did not come.

Beauty waited and waited, ten minutes, twenty minutes, but nothing happened.

"Beast?" she called. "Beast, where are you?"

But there was no answer.

Beauty was very frightened, and she ran through the palace. She ran from room to room, calling his name. "Beast! Beast!" But the palace was empty and silent.

Then she remembered her dream. "The garden!" she said, and she ran outside into the dark.

She ran along the paths, past the roses, to the canal. And there he was: the Beast was lying on the grass by the water. His eyes were closed, and he did not move.

"Oh, no!" cried Beauty. "Beast!"

She fell on her knees next to him. She put her hand on his chest, and his heart was still beating, but very slowly. She ran to the canal and got some water in her hands, and she threw the water on his face.

The Beast opened his eyes. "Beauty," he said weakly. "You forgot your promise, and I was so sad without you. I stopped eating, because I wanted to die. But now I can see you one last time, so I can die happy."

"No, dear Beast!" said Beauty. "You are not going to die! You are going to live, and you are going to be my husband. I thought you were only my friend, but I was wrong. I love you, and I can't live without you."

At that moment, the palace was suddenly full of light. Fireworks went up into the sky and music began to play. Beauty looked up at the lights in surprise.

Then she looked down again, and the Beast was gone! In his place there was a handsome young prince. He was smiling at her.

"Thank you, Beauty," he said, "because you broke the spell."

"But where is my Beast?" cried Beauty.

"I am your Beast," said the prince. "A wicked fairy put a spell on me, and she turned me into a beast. I had to stay ugly forever. Only one thing could break the spell: a good girl had to agree to marry me. And the fairy did not let me show my mind, so I had to look stupid too. You were the only person in the world who saw my good heart."

Beauty gave him her hand, and they walked together into the palace.

In the great hall, Beauty saw her father and all her family. Next to them stood the beautiful lady from her dream, and she was a good fairy.

"Beauty," said the fairy, "you chose a good heart over a handsome face and a clever mind. Now you have all three, and you are going to be a great queen."

Then the fairy turned to the two sisters. "I know your hearts," she said. "They are full of jealousy, so you are going to become two stone statues. You are going to stand at the gate of your sister's palace, and every day you are going to see her happiness. You are going to stay stone until you see your mistakes."

The sisters became statues at once.

The fairy waved her hand, and everyone went to the prince's kingdom. The people of the kingdom were very happy to see their prince again.

The next day Beauty married the prince, and the garden was full of roses, red and pink and white. They lived together for a long, long time, and they were happy, because their love was built on goodness.

The End
