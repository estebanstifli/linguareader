Chapter 1: The Boy at the Window

All children grow up, except one.

The Darling family lived in a tall house in London. There was Mr Darling, who worked in an office in the city. There was Mrs Darling, who had a kind face and a sweet, secret smile. And there were three children: Wendy, John and Michael.

Wendy was the oldest, and she liked stories and liked to tell them. John was the middle child, and he wore glasses and a tall hat, and he wanted to be very important. Michael was the baby. He was only five years old, and he still carried his teddy bear everywhere.

The Darlings were not rich, so they could not pay for a nurse. But they had Nana, a big black and white dog, and she was the best nurse in London. She gave the children their baths, and she carried their medicine in her mouth. She walked with them to school every day, with an umbrella in case of rain.

"A dog for a nurse!" said the neighbours. "How strange!"

But the children loved Nana, and Nana loved them.

Every night, after the children went to sleep, Mrs Darling looked into their minds. All good mothers do this, and she tidied their thoughts like clothes in a drawer. She put the bad thoughts at the bottom and the nice thoughts at the top.

One night she found a strange word in Wendy's mind. The word was "Peter", and she did not know it.

"Who is Peter?" she asked Wendy the next morning.

"Peter Pan, of course," said Wendy. "He lives with the fairies, and sometimes he comes into our room at night. He sits at the end of my bed and plays his pipes."

"Nonsense, darling," said Mrs Darling. "Nobody can come in. We are three floors up."

"There are leaves on the floor," said Wendy. "Look, he brings them on his feet."

Mrs Darling looked. There were some leaves on the floor, but they were not from any tree in London. She did not understand it, and she felt a little cold.

A week later, Mrs Darling sat by the fire in the nursery. The children were asleep, the night was warm and the window was open. Slowly, her eyes closed.

Suddenly, she woke up, and there was a boy in the room! He was dressed in leaves, and he still had all his first teeth. With him was a tiny light, no bigger than your hand, and it flew round and round the room like a bee.

Mrs Darling screamed, the door opened, and Nana ran in. She jumped at the boy, and the boy jumped out of the window.

"Oh, no!" cried Mrs Darling. "He is going to fall!"

She ran to the window and looked down into the street. But there was no boy, so she looked up into the dark sky. She saw only a small star, moving fast.

Then she turned, and Nana was standing in the middle of the room, and there was something in her mouth. It was the boy's shadow! Nana caught it at the window, and it came off in her teeth.

Mrs Darling held the shadow up, and it was very ordinary, like any shadow. She rolled it up carefully and put it in a drawer.

"He is going to come back for it," she thought. "I am sure of that."

Chapter 2: Peter Comes Back

The next Friday, Mr and Mrs Darling went out to a party. Before they left, there was trouble in the nursery.

Michael did not want to take his medicine, and Mr Darling wanted to show him how to be brave. But he did not want to take his own medicine either, so he played a trick. He put his medicine into Nana's bowl. Nana drank it, and then she looked at him with sad eyes.

The children did not laugh, and Mr Darling felt silly and angry.

"That dog is not going to sleep in the nursery!" he shouted. "She is going to sleep outside in the garden!"

He pulled Nana out of the house and tied her up in the yard, and Nana barked and barked.

"She is trying to tell us something," said Mrs Darling. "Something is wrong."

But Mr Darling did not listen, and they put on their coats and went to the party. The nursery was dark, and the three children were asleep.

Then a little light flew in through the window. It was a fairy called Tinker Bell, and she was looking for something. She opened the drawers, one after another.

Then a boy flew in after her, and it was Peter Pan.

"Tink," he whispered. "Where is my shadow?"

Tinker Bell pointed to a drawer. Peter opened it and took out his shadow, and he was very happy. He tried to stick it back on his feet with soap, but it did not work. The shadow fell off again.

Peter sat on the floor and cried.

His crying woke Wendy, and she sat up in bed.

"Boy," she said politely, "why are you crying?"

Peter stood up and bowed to her, and Wendy bowed back from the bed.

"What is your name?" he asked.

"Wendy Moira Angela Darling," she said. "What is yours?"

"Peter Pan."

"Where do you live?"

"Second to the right," said Peter, "and then straight on till morning."

"What a funny address!" said Wendy. "And why are you crying?"

"My shadow does not stick," said Peter.

"Soap is no good," said Wendy. "We must sew it on." She took out her little sewing box. "Sit down, because this is going to hurt a little."

She sewed the shadow onto his feet. Peter was very brave and did not cry. Then he jumped up and danced, and his shadow danced with him.

"How clever I am!" he shouted. "Oh, how clever I am!"

"You are not very polite," said Wendy, "because I did all the work."

"Wendy," said Peter quickly, "one girl is more use than twenty boys."

Wendy smiled. "Would you like a kiss?" she asked.

Peter held out his hand, because he did not know what a kiss was. So Wendy gave him a thimble, the little metal hat for her finger. Peter gave her a button from his clothes, and Wendy put it on a chain round her neck.

"How old are you, Peter?" she asked.

"I do not know," said Peter. "I ran away on the day I was born. I heard my father and mother talking about my future, and they wanted me to be a man. But I want to be a little boy for ever and have fun. So I ran away to live with the fairies."

"Do you live with them now?"

"No, I live with the Lost Boys. They are children who fell out of their prams when their nurses were not looking. Nobody came for them, so they came to the Neverland, and I am their captain."

"What fun!" said Wendy.

"But we have no mother," said Peter, "and nobody tells us stories. Wendy, come with me! You can tell us stories and tuck us in at night."

"But I cannot fly," said Wendy.

"I can teach you," said Peter.

Chapter 3: Second to the Right

Wendy woke John and Michael.

"Get up!" she said, "Peter Pan is here, and he is going to teach us to fly!"

John jumped out of bed at once. Michael rubbed his eyes and looked at the strange boy.

"You must think happy thoughts," said Peter, "lovely, wonderful thoughts, and they lift you up into the air."

The children thought happy thoughts and jumped from the beds, but they all fell down onto the floor.

Peter laughed, and then he blew some fairy dust over them. It was gold and it shone in the dark.

"Now try," he said.

Michael tried first, and he jumped and did not come down. "I am flying!" he shouted. Then John flew, and then Wendy, and they flew round and round the nursery, past the lamp and over the beds.

"Let us go out!" cried John.

Down in the yard, Nana pulled and pulled at her rope. At last she broke it, and she ran to the party to find Mr and Mrs Darling. They ran home as fast as they could, but they were too late. When they opened the nursery door, the window was open and the beds were empty.

The children flew over the roofs of London, past the church towers and over the river. Then they flew out over the sea.

They flew for a long, long time. When they were hungry, Peter took food from the mouths of birds. When they were sleepy, they sometimes fell. Peter caught them, but only at the last moment, because he thought it was funny.

"Peter does not care about us very much," whispered John.

"Be careful what you say," whispered Wendy, "because we do not know the way home without him."

At last, Peter pointed down. "There it is," he said, "the Neverland."

The children saw a green island in a golden sea. There were mountains and forests. There was a lagoon where mermaids played, and a ship with black sails. The children knew the island at once, because they often saw it in their dreams.

"Who is on that ship?" asked John.

"Pirates," said Peter. "Their captain is James Hook, and he is the worst pirate of them all. I cut off his right hand in a fight. Now he has an iron hook instead of a hand, and he uses it like a claw."

Michael started to cry, and Wendy held his hand tightly.

Just then, there was a loud BOOM! The pirates saw them and fired their big gun at them. The children were thrown in different directions through the air.

Wendy was alone, and only Tinker Bell was with her.

"Follow me," said Tink in her fairy language, which sounded like little bells.

Wendy followed her. But Tinker Bell was not a nice fairy that night. She was jealous of Wendy, because Peter liked her. So she flew ahead to the Lost Boys.

The Lost Boys were in the forest, and there were six of them: Tootles, Nibs, Slightly, Curly and the Twins. They wore the skins of animals they killed, and they looked like little bears.

"Peter wants you to shoot the Wendy bird!" Tinker Bell told them. "Quick!"

Tootles had a bow and arrow, and he shot at the big white bird in the sky. Wendy fell down to the ground, with an arrow in her chest.

Chapter 4: The Little House

The Lost Boys ran to look at the bird.

"It is not a bird," said Slightly slowly. "I think it is a lady."

"A lady!" said Tootles, and he went white. "Oh, no, what did I do?"

At that moment, Peter flew down. "Great news, boys!" he shouted. "I brought a mother for you all!"

Nobody said anything, and then Tootles stepped forward. "Look, Peter," he said quietly. "I shot her."

Peter looked at Wendy on the ground, and he took the arrow from Tootles. He was very angry.

"Strike, Peter," said Tootles, "strike me."

But Peter could not do it, because at that moment Wendy's arm moved.

"She is alive!" cried Nibs.

Peter knelt down beside her. The arrow did not go into her heart. It hit the button on the chain round her neck, his button.

"Look," said Peter, "it is the kiss I gave her, and it saved her life."

Then they heard the sound of little bells, because Tinker Bell was crying in the trees.

"Tink!" said Peter. "You did this! I am not your friend any more, so go away for ever!"

Tinker Bell was very sad, and Wendy lifted her arm a little.

"Oh, please," said the boys to Peter. "Wendy wants you to forgive her."

"All right," said Peter, "not for ever, but go away for a whole week."

Then the boys had a problem. Wendy was hurt, and they could not move her. But Peter had an idea.

"We are going to build a house round her," he said.

The boys ran into the forest, and they came back with branches, leaves and big flowers. John and Michael arrived too, tired after their long walk. Soon there was a little house round Wendy, with red walls and a green roof. John's hat was the chimney, and smoke came out of it.

Peter knocked politely on the little door, and Wendy opened it.

"Wendy lady," said the boys, "please be our mother."

"I am only a little girl," said Wendy, "and I know nothing about being a mother."

"That does not matter," said Peter. "What we need is a nice motherly person."

"Oh, well," said Wendy, "I feel that is exactly what I am. Come inside, you naughty children. I am going to tell you a story, and then it is bedtime."

So began a happy life. The Lost Boys lived in a home under the ground. To get in, each boy jumped into a hollow tree. Every boy had his own tree, and Wendy and her brothers got their own trees too. In the room under the ground there was a big fire. The boys slept together in one large bed. When one boy wanted to turn over, Wendy blew a whistle, and they all turned over together.

Wendy cooked and sewed all day, and she was always busy. The boys tore holes in their socks, and she mended them.

"Oh, dear," she sometimes said, "I think women without children are lucky."

But she said it with a happy face.

Every night she told them stories, and their favourite was about a mother and a father and their three children. One night the children flew away to the Neverland, but their mother always kept the window open for them.

Peter did not like that story. "Mothers do not wait," he said. "Long ago, I flew home to my mother, but the window was closed, and there was another little boy sleeping in my bed."

The boys went quiet, Michael began to cry, and even John looked worried.

Chapter 5: The Mermaids' Lagoon

On the island, there was always adventure.

One day the children went to the lagoon. It was a beautiful place with blue water, and mermaids sat on the rocks there, combing their long hair. The boys tried to talk to them, but the mermaids only splashed them with their tails and laughed. They talked only to Peter.

In the middle of the lagoon there was a big black rock. Its name was Marooners' Rock, and pirates left bad sailors there. When the water came up, the sailors drowned.

The children were lying in the sun on Marooners' Rock. Suddenly, the sun went away and the water became cold.

"Pirates!" cried Peter. "Dive!"

The boys jumped into the water and hid. A little boat came towards the rock, and in it were two pirates, Smee and Starkey. With them was a young princess with dark hair. Her hands and feet were tied with rope. It was Tiger Lily.

Tiger Lily was the daughter of a great chief. Her people lived in the forest, and they were friends of the Lost Boys. Now the pirates wanted to leave her on the rock. The water was coming up, and soon she could not breathe.

But Tiger Lily was proud, and she did not cry and she did not ask for help.

Peter was angry. He was hiding behind the rock, and he called out in Captain Hook's voice, because he could copy any voice.

"Ahoy there, you silly sailors!" he shouted. "Set her free!"

"But Captain..." said Smee.

"Cut her ropes, or I am going to put my hook in you!"

The pirates were afraid, so they cut Tiger Lily's ropes, and she swam away into the dark water like a fish.

Then there was a real shout across the water. "Boat ahoy!"

It was the real Captain Hook! He swam to the boat and climbed in, and his face was dark and angry.

"Where is the princess?" he asked.

"We let her go, Captain," said Smee, "because you told us to."

Hook went white. "I said nothing of the kind!" he cried. "Spirit that haunts this lagoon, can you hear me?"

"Odds, bobs, hammer and tongs," said Peter in Hook's voice. "I hear you."

"Who are you?" asked Hook.

"I am James Hook, captain of the Jolly Roger."

"You are not! You are not!" shouted Hook, and then he had an idea. "Are you a fish? Are you a man? Are you a boy?"

"Yes!" said Peter.

"A wonderful boy?"

"Yes!" said Peter proudly, because he could not stop himself. "I am Peter Pan!"

Now Hook knew, and there was a great fight. The pirates fought the boys in the water. Peter and Hook climbed up the rock at the same moment. Peter took Hook's knife, but Hook was lower down, and it was not fair to fight that way. So Peter gave him his hand to help him up.

Hook bit his hand, and then he hit him with his iron hook.

Peter was shocked. It was not the pain, it was the unfairness. Every child feels like this the first time.

Just then, they heard a sound: tick, tock, tick, tock. Hook jumped into the water and swam to his ship as fast as he could.

The boys swam home, but Peter and Wendy were left on the rock. Peter was hurt and Wendy was tired. The water came up higher and higher.

"We are going to drown," said Wendy.

A kite flew past, and Peter caught its tail. "It can carry one of us," he said, "so take it, Wendy."

The kite lifted Wendy into the air and carried her away. Peter was alone, and the water came up to his feet. He was afraid for a moment, but then he smiled.

"To die is going to be an awfully big adventure," he said.

Then a big bird came, swimming on her nest on the water. It was the Never bird, and she pushed the nest to the rock and flew away. Peter took her eggs and put them in Starkey's hat, which was floating on the water. Then he sat in the nest, used his shirt as a sail, and sailed home.

Chapter 6: The Ticking Crocodile

On his ship, Captain Hook sat alone. He was a tall man with long black hair and cold blue eyes. He was very well dressed. He was polite, even when he was cruel. And he hated Peter Pan more than anything in the world.

"Smee," he said, "why does the crocodile follow me?"

Smee knew the story, because all the pirates knew it.

Long ago, Peter cut off Hook's right hand in a fight and threw it into the sea. A crocodile was swimming past, and it ate the hand. It liked the taste very much. After that, the crocodile followed Hook everywhere, because it wanted to eat the rest of him.

But one day the crocodile swallowed a clock. Now, when it came near, Hook could hear the clock inside it: tick, tock, tick, tock.

"One day the clock is going to stop," said Hook. "And then the crocodile is going to get me."

He was afraid, and he was angry, too.

"Peter Pan is too proud," he said, "but now he has a mother. I am going to catch them all, and Wendy is going to be our mother. And the boys are going to walk the plank."

The plank was a long piece of wood over the side of the ship. Pirates made their prisoners walk along it until they fell into the sea.

Meanwhile, under the ground, the children were having dinner. Since the day at the lagoon, Tiger Lily and her people were great friends of the boys. Every night the warriors sat above the house and watched for pirates. They called Peter "the Great Father", and Peter liked that very much.

After dinner, Wendy told a story, and it was the story of the mother and the open window again.

"The mother loved her children," she said, "and she knew they were going to come home one day. So every night, she left the window open."

This time Wendy stopped and looked at John and Michael. John could not remember his father's face. Michael could not remember anything about his home. Michael thought Wendy was his real mother.

Wendy was suddenly afraid.

"We must go home," she said. "Now, before it is too late. Our mother is waiting for us."

"Yes," said John, "we must go."

The Lost Boys were sad, and Nibs asked a question. "Can we come too?" he said. "Can your mother be our mother?"

"Yes," said Wendy, "I am sure she is going to love you. Peter, are you coming too?"

"No," said Peter. He was angry, but he did not show it. "If I go to your house, they are going to make me grow up. I do not want to go to school, and I do not want to learn serious things. I want to be a little boy for ever and have fun."

"Please, Peter," said Wendy.

"No," he said, "but I am going to help you. Tink can show you the way across the sea."

Tinker Bell was in her little room in the wall. She was not happy, but she agreed.

The boys put on their coats and got ready, and Wendy gave them each a kiss.

Then suddenly, they heard terrible noises above their heads. There were shouts and screams and the sound of swords. The pirates were attacking the warriors!

The children listened in the dark, and the noise was terrible. Then everything went quiet.

"Who won?" whispered Wendy.

"If the warriors won, they are going to beat the drum," said Peter, "because that is their sign."

Above them, Hook took the drum and smiled a cruel smile. Then he beat it, two times.

"The warriors won!" shouted the boys happily.

Chapter 7: Tinker Bell's Light

One by one, the boys climbed up their hollow trees. One by one, the pirates caught them as they came out. The pirates tied them up like parcels and carried them down to the ship. Wendy came last, and Hook took off his hat and bowed to her politely. Then they took her too.

Only Peter stayed under the ground, asleep in the big bed.

Hook did not go with his men, and he waited by Peter's tree. Then he climbed down into the house very quietly. He saw Peter sleeping, and on the shelf there was a little glass of medicine. Wendy always made Peter take it at bedtime.

Hook smiled and took a small bottle out of his coat. It was poison, the worst poison in the world. He put five drops into Peter's medicine. Then he went away into the dark forest.

Some time later, Peter woke up, because there was a knock at the door.

"Who is there?" he asked.

"Let me in, Peter!" said a voice like little bells, and it was Tinker Bell.

She flew in, very excited, and she told him everything. "The pirates caught Wendy and the boys!" she cried. "They are on the ship!"

"I am going to rescue her!" said Peter. He took his sword, and then he saw his medicine. "But first, I must take my medicine, because Wendy wants me to."

"No!" said Tinker Bell. "It is poison! Hook was here, and I heard him."

"Nonsense," said Peter, "Hook was never here."

He lifted the glass, but Tinker Bell was faster. She flew between his lips and the glass, and she drank all the medicine.

"Tink! Why did you drink my medicine?"

Tinker Bell did not answer. She flew in a slow, strange way to her little room. Her light was getting weaker and weaker.

"It was poison, Peter," she said in a tiny voice, "and now I am going to die."

"Oh, Tink, you saved my life!" said Peter, and he knelt by her little bed. "What can I do?"

"I think I can get well," she whispered, "if children believe in fairies."

Peter stood up. It was night, and all over the world children were dreaming of the Neverland. Peter spoke to all of them, and perhaps he spoke to you, too.

"Do you believe?" he cried. "If you believe in fairies, clap your hands! Do not let Tink die!"

Many children clapped, but some did not. A few were too mean to clap. But it was enough. Tinker Bell's light became stronger, and soon she was flying round the room again, ringing like bells. She was very rude and very happy.

"Now," said Peter, "to rescue Wendy!"

He flew out into the night. The moon was bright over the island. The pirates were gone, and the forest was quiet. On the way, he met the crocodile, which was swimming towards the ship too. But this time there was no sound. The clock inside it was finally quiet.

Peter had a clever idea. "I am going to tick like the crocodile," he said, "and then nobody is going to stop me."

So he swam to the ship, ticking all the way: tick, tock, tick, tock.

On the ship, Hook was standing by the plank. The boys stood in a line, tied with ropes. Wendy was tied to the mast, and the pirates were laughing.

Then they heard it: tick, tock, tick, tock.

Hook fell to the floor. "Hide me!" he cried, "the crocodile is here!"

Chapter 8: The Window Is Open

The pirates and the boys looked over the side of the ship. They did not see a crocodile, but they saw Peter Pan climbing up the side. He put his finger to his lips, and the boys said nothing.

Peter ran into the captain's cabin and hid there. A pirate went in to look for him. There was a scream, and then nothing. Another pirate went in. Again there was a scream.

"It is a ghost!" cried the pirates. "The ship is cursed!"

Then Peter jumped out of the cabin. "The boys are free!" he shouted. He cut their ropes with his knife. "Now fight, boys!"

It was a wonderful battle, and the boys had swords and knives from the cabin. They fought the pirates up and down the deck. The pirates were big, but the boys were quick. One by one, the pirates fell into the sea.

At last, only Hook was left. He fought with his sword in his left hand, and Peter fought with his little knife.

"Proud and wicked boy," said Hook. "Prepare to meet your end."

"Dark and sinister man," said Peter. "Have at you!"

They fought for a long time. Peter was quick, like the wind. He danced round Hook, and the captain could not touch him. Hook was tired, and he knew he was going to lose.

"Who are you, Pan?" he cried. "What are you?"

"I am youth," said Peter, "I am joy. I am a little bird that broke out of the egg."

Hook climbed up onto the side of the ship. Below, the crocodile was waiting with its mouth open. It was not ticking any more, but it was there.

Peter did not use his knife. He kicked Hook instead, and that was bad manners. "Bad form!" cried Hook with a strange, happy smile. Then he fell into the sea, and the crocodile was waiting for him.

That was the end of Captain Hook.

The children cheered, and Wendy hugged all the boys. That night, Peter slept on the ship. He dreamed and cried in his sleep, and Wendy held him close.

The next day, Peter was the captain of the Jolly Roger, and he wore Hook's clothes and he walked like Hook. The children sailed home across the sea, and then they flew the last part of the way over London.

In the Darling house, the nursery window was open. Every night since the children left, Mrs Darling sat by the window and waited. Mr Darling was very sorry for his trick with Nana. So he lived in Nana's dog house, and he went to the office in it every day. People in the street laughed, but he did not care.

Peter and Tink arrived first. Peter wanted to close the window. "Then Wendy is going to think her mother forgot her," he said, "and she is going to stay with me."

But then he saw Mrs Darling, who was sitting by the fire, and there were tears in her eyes. Peter was not a bad boy, so he opened the window again.

"Come on, Tink," he said sadly. "We do not want silly mothers."

He flew away, and Wendy, John and Michael flew in and climbed into their beds. Mrs Darling woke up and saw them, but she did not believe her eyes. Then she held out her arms, and the three children ran into them.

Nana barked with joy, and Mr Darling came home and cried happily.

Then the six Lost Boys came in. "Please," said Wendy, "can they stay with us?"

Mrs Darling said yes at once. Mr Darling said there were too many, but then he laughed and said yes too.

"Peter," said Mrs Darling at the window, "you can stay too. We can send you to school, and you can work in an office."

"Will I be a man one day?" asked Peter.

"Yes, very soon."

"Then no," said Peter, "nobody is going to catch me and make me a man. I want to be a little boy for ever and have fun."

"Then may Wendy come to you every spring?" asked Mrs Darling. "She can clean your little house."

Peter was happy with that. "Yes!" he said, and he flew away.

He came the next spring, but not the spring after, because he forgot. Peter always forgot things.

Years went by. The Lost Boys grew up and went to school and then to offices. Wendy grew up too. She married and had a little girl called Jane.

One night, Peter came back to the window, and he did not look older at all. He still had all his first teeth.

"Wendy," he said, "it is spring cleaning time."

"I cannot come, Peter," said Wendy sadly, "because I am a grown-up now."

But Jane was awake. She looked at the boy in the window with big eyes.

"I can come," said Jane, "and I can be your mother."

So Jane flew away with Peter, second to the right and straight on till morning. And when Jane grows up, she is going to have a daughter, and Peter is going to come for her too. It is going to go on like this for ever, as long as children are happy and innocent and heartless.

The End
