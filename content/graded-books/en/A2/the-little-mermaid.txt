Chapter 1: The Palace Under the Sea

Far out in the ocean, the water is as blue as a cornflower and as clear as glass. It is very, very deep there. No anchor can reach the bottom.

Down on the white sand, strange trees and plants grow. Their long leaves move with the water, as if they are alive. Fish swim between the branches, just like birds in our gardens.

In the deepest place stood the palace of the Sea King. Its walls were made of coral, and its tall windows were made of clear amber. The roof was made of shells. Inside every shell lay a shining pearl.

The Sea King lived there with his old mother and his six daughters. His wife was dead, so his mother looked after the house. She was a wise and proud old lady. She wore twelve oysters on her tail. The other great ladies of the sea could only wear six.

"I am the king's mother," she said. "Twelve oysters are right for me."

But she was also very kind, and she loved her six granddaughters. They were all beautiful sea princesses. The youngest was the most beautiful of all. Her skin was as soft as a rose petal. Her eyes were as blue as the deepest sea. But, like her sisters, she had no feet. Her body ended in a fish's tail.

All day long, the princesses played in the great halls of the palace. When the windows were open, the fish swam in. They came to the princesses and ate from their hands.

Outside the palace there was a big garden. The fruit shone like gold, and the flowers looked like little flames. Each princess had her own piece of the garden. One sister made her garden in the shape of a whale. Another made hers in the shape of a mermaid. But the youngest made her garden round, like the sun. She planted only red flowers there, as red as the sun at evening.

The youngest princess was a quiet child. She often sat alone and thought. Her sisters loved the funny things from old ships at the bottom of the sea. But the youngest wanted only one thing. It was a statue of white marble. It showed a handsome boy, and it came down to the sea floor after a shipwreck. She put it next to her red flowers. Then she planted a pink tree next to it. Its branches hung over the boy, and its blue shadows danced on the sand.

Nothing made her happier than her grandmother's stories about the world up above.

"Tell me about the ships again," she said. "Tell me about the towns, the people and the animals."

"Up there, the flowers have a sweet smell," said her grandmother. "There is no smell down here. The trees in the forests are green. And the fish in the branches sing! They sing so beautifully that it is a joy to hear them."

The grandmother meant the birds. But the little princess never saw a bird, so she called them fish.

"When you are fifteen," said the grandmother, "you may swim up to the top of the sea. You may sit on the rocks in the moonlight and watch the big ships. You will see forests and towns."

"Fifteen!" said the youngest princess. "That is so far away!"

Chapter 2: The Five Sisters

The next year, the eldest sister was fifteen. The sisters were born one year apart, so the youngest had to wait five more years.

"When you come back, you must tell me everything," she said to the eldest sister.

"I promise," said the eldest sister.

At night the youngest often stood at the open window and looked up through the dark blue water. She could see the moon and the stars. They were pale, but they looked much bigger through the water. Sometimes a black shadow moved across them. Then she knew it was a whale, or a ship full of people. The people never thought of the little mermaid down below, holding up her white arms towards their ship.

The eldest sister came back with a hundred things to tell.

"The best thing," she said, "is to lie on a sandbank in the moonlight. You can see a big town near the coast. Its lights shine like a hundred stars. You can hear music and people. And you can hear the church bells ringing."

That night the youngest stood at her window again. She thought about the big town, and she almost heard the bells ringing down to her.

The next year, the second sister went up just as the sun was going down.

"The sky looked like gold," she said. "The clouds were red and purple. A line of wild swans flew past, like a long white ribbon. I swam after them, but then the sun went down."

The year after, it was the third sister's turn. She was the bravest of them all. She swam up a wide river.

"I saw green hills, castles and great forests," she said. "In a little bay I found some human children. They were swimming, and they had no tails! I wanted to play with them, but they ran away. Then a little black animal came to the water. It was a dog, but I never saw a dog before. It barked at me so loudly that I was frightened, and I swam away."

The fourth sister was not so brave. She stayed out in the middle of the sea.

"You can see for miles around you," she said. "The sky is like a great glass bell. The dolphins turned somersaults, and the whales blew water out of their noses. It looked like a hundred fountains."

The fifth sister's birthday was in winter. The sea was green, and great icebergs floated in it.

"Each one looked like a pearl," she said, "but it was bigger than a church tower. I sat on the biggest one. Then a storm came, and lightning flashed. The people on the ships were very frightened. But I sat calmly on my iceberg and watched the blue lightning."

The first time, each sister was full of joy. But after a month or two, the world above was not so exciting any more. Soon they all said that home was the best place.

Still, in the evenings the five sisters often swam up together, arm in arm. They had beautiful voices, more beautiful than any human voice. When a storm was coming, they swam in front of the ships and sang.

"Do not be afraid to come down to us," they sang.

But the sailors thought it was the noise of the storm. And when a ship sank, the people drowned. They only came to the Sea King's palace as dead bodies.

When her sisters swam up together, the youngest stayed alone. She wanted to cry. But a mermaid has no tears, and that makes her sadness even greater.

"Oh, I wish I was fifteen!" she said. "I know I will love the world up there, and the people who live in it."

Chapter 3: The Ship and the Storm

At last she was fifteen.

"Now you are grown up too," said her grandmother. "Let me make you beautiful, like your sisters."

She put a crown of white lilies on the girl's hair. Then she put eight big oysters on the princess's tail.

"Ow! That hurts!" said the little mermaid.

"Yes," said her grandmother. "You must suffer a little to be beautiful."

"Goodbye!" called the little mermaid, and she rose up through the water, as light as a bubble.

When she lifted her head out of the sea, the sun was just going down. The clouds were still pink and gold. The air was soft, and the sea was calm.

Not far away, there was a big ship with three masts. There was music and singing on the ship. When it got dark, the sailors lit hundreds of coloured lanterns.

The little mermaid swam right up to the cabin window. Each time a wave lifted her, she could look through the glass. Inside there were many people in beautiful clothes. The most handsome of them was a young prince with large dark eyes. It was his sixteenth birthday, and that was why there was a party.

When the prince came out on deck, a hundred rockets flew up into the sky. The little mermaid was so frightened that she dived under the water. But soon she put her head out again. Now it looked as if all the stars were falling down to her. She never saw fireworks before.

And how handsome the young prince was! He shook hands with everybody. He smiled and laughed while the music played.

It was late, but the little mermaid could not take her eyes off him. Then the ship began to move faster. The waves grew higher. Big black clouds came, and lightning flashed. A terrible storm was coming.

"Take down the sails!" shouted the sailors.

The waves rose up like black mountains. The ship dived down between them like a swan, and then it rose up again. The little mermaid thought it was fun. But the sailors did not think so.

The ship cracked and groaned. The main mast broke in the middle like a stick. The ship fell on its side, and the water rushed in.

Now the little mermaid understood that the people were in danger. For a moment it was so dark that she could see nothing. Then the lightning flashed, and she looked for the young prince. She saw him fall into the deep sea.

At first she was happy. "Now he is coming down to me," she thought.

But then she remembered that humans cannot live in the water.

"No! He must not die!" she said.

She swam between the broken pieces of the ship. They could easily crush her, but she did not think about that. At last she reached the young prince. His arms and legs were very weak, and his beautiful eyes were closed. He was going to die.

But the little mermaid held his head above the water. Then she let the waves carry them both.

Chapter 4: The Girl by the Church

In the morning the storm was over. There was nothing left of the ship. The sun rose red and bright out of the sea, but the prince's eyes stayed closed.

The little mermaid kissed his forehead and stroked back his wet hair.

"He looks just like the marble statue in my garden," she thought. "Please live," she said. "Please wake up."

Then she saw land in front of her. There were high blue mountains with white snow on top. Near the coast stood a big white building. Maybe it was a church, or a school. Lemon and orange trees grew in its garden.

There was a little bay with fine white sand. The little mermaid swam there with the prince. She laid him on the warm sand, with his head in the sunshine.

Then bells began to ring in the white building, and some young girls came out into the garden. The little mermaid quickly swam away. She hid behind some rocks and covered her hair with sea foam. Then she watched to see who came to the poor prince.

Soon a young girl walked down to the beach. She saw the prince, and she ran to get more people. The prince opened his eyes. He smiled at the people around him. But he did not smile at the little mermaid. He did not know that she saved his life.

She felt very sad. She dived under the water and swam slowly home.

"What did you see up there?" her sisters asked.

But she did not tell them anything.

Many evenings and many mornings, she swam up to the place where she left the prince. She watched the snow melt on the mountains. But she never saw the prince, so she always came home sadder than before.

Her only joy was to sit in her garden. She put her arms around the marble statue, because it looked like the prince. She did not look after her flowers any more, and they grew wild over the paths.

At last she told her secret to one of her sisters. Soon the other sisters knew too. And they told a few mermaid friends. One of these friends knew about the prince.

"I know who he is," she said, "and I know where his palace is."

"Come, little sister!" said the princesses. In a long line, arm in arm, they swam to the prince's palace.

It was made of a light yellow stone, with golden roofs. Wide marble stairs went right down into the sea. Through the tall windows, you could see beautiful rooms with silk curtains and great paintings.

Now she knew where he lived. After that, she went there on many evenings. She swam much nearer to the land than her sisters. She sat under the marble balcony and looked at the young prince. He thought he was alone in the moonlight.

At night, the fishermen went out to sea with their torches. She often heard them talking about the young prince.

"He is a good man," said one fisherman.

"Yes, the best," said another. "He is kind to everybody."

The little mermaid was happy to hear it. "I saved his life," she thought. "I held his head on the waves." But he knew nothing about it.

Chapter 5: The Question of the Soul

The little mermaid loved humans more and more. Their world seemed much bigger than hers. They could sail over the sea in ships. They could climb the high mountains, far above the clouds.

There were so many things she wanted to know. So she went to her grandmother.

"Grandmother," she said, "when humans do not drown, do they live forever?"

"No, they die," said the old lady. "And their lives are even shorter than ours. We can live for three hundred years. But when we die, we turn into foam on the water. We have no soul that lives forever. We are like the green seaweed. Once it is cut, it can never grow again."

"And humans?" asked the little mermaid.

"Humans have a soul," said her grandmother. "It lives forever, even after their body dies. It rises up through the clear air, up to the shining stars."

"Why do we not have a soul?" asked the little mermaid sadly. "I want to give all my three hundred years to be a human for just one day."

"You must not think like that," said the old lady. "We are much happier than the people up there."

"So I am going to die and float on the sea as foam," said the little mermaid. "Is there nothing I can do to get a soul?"

"There is only one way," said her grandmother. "A man must love you more than his father and his mother. A priest must put his right hand into yours, and he must promise to be true to you forever. Then his soul will flow into your body. He can give you a soul and still keep his own."

The little mermaid's eyes shone.

"But that can never happen," said her grandmother. "Your fish's tail is beautiful here in the sea. But up on land they think it is ugly. Up there, you must have two clumsy things that they call legs."

The little mermaid sighed and looked sadly at her tail.

"Let us be happy," said the old lady. "Let us dance and sing for our three hundred years. Tonight we will have a ball at the palace."

It was a wonderful ball. The walls of the great hall were made of clear glass. Hundreds of giant shells stood along the walls, with blue fire burning inside them. The light shone out into the sea, and all the fish swam towards the glass.

The mermen and mermaids danced to their own beautiful songs. The little mermaid sang the most beautifully of all. Everyone clapped for her, and for a moment she was happy. She knew that she had the loveliest voice on land and in the sea.

But soon she began to think about the prince again. So she quietly left the ball and sat sadly in her garden. Then she heard the sound of a horn coming down through the water.

"He is sailing up there now," she thought. "I will do anything to win him and a soul that lives forever. While my sisters are dancing, I will go to the sea witch. I am very afraid of her. But maybe she can help me."

Chapter 6: The Sea Witch

The little mermaid swam towards the wild whirlpools. The sea witch lived behind them. No flowers grew there, only bare grey sand. The water turned round and round, and it pulled everything down into the deep.

Behind the whirlpools stood the witch's house, in the middle of a strange forest. The trees were half animal and half plant. They looked like snakes with a hundred heads. Their branches were long, slimy arms with fingers like worms. They took hold of everything they could reach, and they never let it go.

The little mermaid stopped. Her heart was beating fast with fear. But then she thought about the prince, and she felt brave again.

She tied her long hair tightly around her head. Then she shot forward through the water, as fast as a fish. The ugly arms reached out for her on every side. Each of them held something tightly: the white bones of drowned sailors, ships' wheels and boxes. And one held a little mermaid. That was the most terrible thing of all.

At last she came to the witch's house. It was made of the bones of shipwrecked people. There sat the sea witch. A big toad was eating from her mouth, and fat water snakes crawled all over her.

"I know exactly what you want," said the sea witch. "It is very stupid of you. But you will have it, because it will bring you unhappiness, my pretty princess. You want two legs, like humans. Then the young prince can fall in love with you, and you can have him and a soul."

The witch laughed so loudly and so horribly that the toad and the snakes fell to the ground.

"I will make you a drink," she said. "Before the sun comes up, swim to land with it. Sit on the beach and drink it. Then your tail will turn into legs."

The little mermaid listened in silence.

"But it will hurt," said the witch. "It will feel like a sword cutting through you. Everyone who sees you will say that you are the loveliest girl in the world. No dancer will ever move as lightly as you. But every step will feel like walking on sharp knives. Can you suffer all that?"

"Yes," said the little mermaid in a shaking voice.

"But remember," said the witch. "You can never be a mermaid again. You can never go home to your sisters or to your father's palace. And you must win the prince's love. A priest must make you man and wife. If not, you will never have a soul. And on the first morning after he marries another girl, your heart will break. You will become foam on the water."

The little mermaid went as pale as death. "I want to do it," she said.

"But you must pay me too," said the witch. "You have the loveliest voice in the sea. You think you can win the prince with it. But you must give that voice to me."

"But if you take my voice," said the little mermaid, "what will I keep?"

"Your beautiful body," said the witch, "your graceful walk, and your lovely eyes. With those you can easily win a human heart. Put out your little tongue. I will cut it off, and you will have the magic drink."

"Let it be so," said the little mermaid.

The witch put her pot on the fire. She cut her own chest and let her black blood drip into it. The steam rose in strange shapes. When the drink was boiling, it sounded like a crocodile crying. At last it was ready, and it looked like the clearest water.

"There it is," said the witch.

Then she cut off the little mermaid's tongue. Now the little mermaid could not speak or sing.

Chapter 7: Life in the Palace

The little mermaid looked at her father's palace. Everyone was asleep. She took one flower from each of her sisters' gardens. She threw a thousand kisses towards the palace. Then she rose up through the dark blue water.

The sun was not up yet when she came to the prince's marble stairs. She drank the burning drink. It felt like a sword was cutting through her body. The pain was so terrible that she fell down like a dead person.

When the sun shone over the sea, she woke up. The young prince was standing in front of her, looking at her with his dark eyes. She looked down. Her fish's tail was gone, and she had two pretty white legs. But she had no clothes, so she covered herself with her long hair.

"Who are you?" asked the prince. "How did you come here?"

She looked at him softly and sadly with her blue eyes, because she could not speak. He took her by the hand and led her into the palace. Every step felt like walking on sharp knives, just as the witch said. But she walked next to the prince as lightly as a bubble. Everyone was surprised by her lovely walk.

They dressed her in silk. She was the most beautiful girl in the palace. But she could not sing or speak.

One evening, some girls in silk and gold sang for the prince. One girl sang more beautifully than the others, and the prince smiled at her. This made the little mermaid very sad.

"Oh, if only he knew," she thought. "I gave my voice away forever to be near him."

Then the girls danced, and the little mermaid danced too. She lifted her white arms and floated across the floor. Nobody ever danced like her. Everyone loved it, especially the prince.

"You are my little foundling," he said. "You must always stay with me."

She danced again and again, but every step felt like sharp knives. At night she slept outside his door, on a velvet cushion.

The prince gave her boy's clothes, so she could ride with him on horseback. They rode through the green forests, where the birds sang. They climbed high mountains together. Her feet bled, and everyone could see it. But she only laughed and followed him.

At night, when everyone was asleep, she went down the marble stairs. She cooled her burning feet in the cold sea water and thought about her family.

One night her sisters came up, arm in arm, singing sadly. She waved to them.

"We are so sad without you," they said.

After that, they visited her every night. Once she saw her old grandmother far out at sea, and the Sea King with his crown. They held out their hands towards her. But they did not come near the land.

Every day the prince loved her more. But he loved her the way you love a sweet child. He never thought of making her his queen.

"Do you love me more than all the others?" her eyes seemed to say.

"Yes, you are the dearest to me," said the prince. "You have the kindest heart of all. And you look like a girl I saw once."

He told her the story.

"I was on a ship that sank in a storm," he said. "The waves carried me to a beach near a holy temple. A young girl from the temple found me and saved my life. She is the only girl I can ever love. But you look like her. She belongs to the temple, so I can never marry her. But luck sent you to me instead."

"Ah, he does not know that I saved his life," thought the little mermaid. "I carried him over the sea. I saw the girl he loves more than me."

She sighed deeply, because she could not cry.

Chapter 8: The Wedding Ship

Soon there was news. "The prince is going to visit the neighbouring king," people said. "He is going to see the king's daughter. Maybe he will marry her."

"I must go," the prince said to the little mermaid. "My parents want it. But I cannot love this princess. If I ever choose a bride, I will choose you, my silent foundling with the speaking eyes."

He kissed her and played with her long hair, and she dreamed of human happiness and a soul.

They sailed on a great ship. At night, the little mermaid sat by the side of the ship and looked down into the clear water. Her sisters came up and looked at her sadly. She waved to them and smiled.

The next morning, the ship sailed into the harbour of the neighbouring town. All the church bells were ringing, and soldiers stood with flags. Every day there were balls and parties. But the princess was not there yet.

"She is far away in a holy temple," people said. "She is learning to be a good queen."

At last she came. The little mermaid looked at her, and she never saw a lovelier girl. Her deep blue eyes were kind and true.

"It is you!" cried the prince. "You saved me when I lay half dead on the beach!"

He took the blushing princess in his arms.

"Oh, I am so happy," he said to the little mermaid. "My greatest wish is coming true. You will be happy for me, because you love me more than anyone."

The little mermaid kissed his hand. She felt as if her heart was already breaking.

The wedding was in the great church. The little mermaid, in silk and gold, held the bride's long dress. But she did not hear the music. She thought only of her death.

That evening, the bride and groom went on board the ship. In the middle of the deck stood a beautiful tent of purple and gold. When it got dark, the sailors lit coloured lamps and danced. The little mermaid remembered the night of the prince's birthday. So she danced too. She never danced so beautifully. Sharp knives seemed to cut her feet, but the pain in her heart was much worse.

"For him I left my family and my home," she thought. "I gave away my voice. And now this is the last night I will see him."

Long after midnight, the prince and his bride went to rest in the tent. Everything was quiet. The little mermaid looked towards the east, waiting for the first pink light of morning. She knew that the first ray of the sun was going to kill her.

Then she saw her sisters rise out of the sea. They were very pale. Their long, beautiful hair was cut off.

"We gave our hair to the sea witch," they said. "And she gave us this knife. Before the sun rises, you must push it into the prince's heart. When his warm blood falls on your feet, they will grow into a fish's tail. Then you can come home to us and live your three hundred years."

"Hurry!" they said. "Either he dies or you die! Our grandmother is so sad that all her white hair fell out. Look at that red line in the sky! In a few minutes the sun will rise!"

Then they sank under the waves.

The little mermaid pulled back the curtain of the tent. The bride was sleeping with her head on the prince's chest. The little mermaid bent down and kissed his forehead. She looked at the sky. The red light was growing brighter. She looked at the sharp knife. In his dreams, the prince said the name of his bride.

The knife shook in the little mermaid's hand. Then she threw it far out into the waves. The water shone red where it fell. She looked at the prince one last time. Then she threw herself from the ship into the sea, and she felt her body turning into foam.

The sun rose out of the sea. Its rays fell warm and soft on the foam. But the little mermaid did not feel death. Above her, hundreds of lovely, clear shapes were floating in the air. Their voices were like music, but no human ear could hear them. And the little mermaid saw that she had a body like theirs. Slowly, she rose up out of the foam.

"Where am I going?" she asked. Her voice was softer and lovelier than any music on earth.

"To the daughters of the air," they answered. "A mermaid has no soul. The daughters of the air do not have a soul either. But we can win one with good deeds. We fly to hot countries and bring cool winds to sick people. We carry the sweet smell of flowers. When we do good for three hundred years, we get a soul that lives forever."

"Poor little mermaid," they said. "You tried with all your heart. You suffered and you were patient. Now you are one of us. With your good deeds, you can win a soul too."

The little mermaid lifted her clear arms towards the sun. For the first time, she felt tears in her eyes.

On the ship, the prince and his bride were looking for her. They looked sadly into the white foam, as if they knew what happened.

Nobody could see her, but she kissed the bride on the forehead and smiled at the prince. Then, with the other daughters of the air, she rose up onto a pink cloud that was sailing across the sky.

The End
